Title of Procedure: 
Electromyography-Guided Spinal Cord Stimulator Trial for Chronic Neuropathic Pain

Indication for Procedure:

A 55-year-old male presented with chronic neuropathic pain in the lower limbs secondary to failed back surgery syndrome. Clinical examination revealed allodynia and hyperalgesia along the distribution of the affected nerves. Electromyography (EMG) was recommended to confirm nerve involvement and guide targeted intervention.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Chronic neuropathic pain secondary to failed back surgery syndrome.

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Chronic neuropathic pain secondary to failed back surgery syndrome.

Procedure:

The patient was positioned comfortably in a prone position. Following skin preparation, needle electrodes were inserted adjacent to the affected nerves to assess their electrical activity and degree of dysfunction.

Under real-time EMG guidance, temporary electrodes were placed along the epidural space to provide spinal cord stimulation. The patient underwent a trial period to assess the efficacy of spinal cord stimulation in reducing pain. Pain relief and changes in sensory perception were monitored throughout the trial period.

Impression:

EMG findings support the diagnosis of chronic neuropathic pain secondary to failed back surgery syndrome. Electromyography-guided spinal cord stimulator trial performed for targeted pain relief.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was closely monitored during the trial period for changes in pain intensity and sensory perception. Pain diaries were provided to track daily pain levels and functional improvements. Follow-up appointments were scheduled to evaluate the trial outcomes and discuss potential options for permanent spinal cord stimulator implantation.
