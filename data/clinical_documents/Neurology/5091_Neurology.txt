Title of Procedure: 
Therapeutic Lumbar Puncture for Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension

Indication for Procedure:

A 30-year-old obese female presented with chronic headaches, visual disturbances, and papilledema on fundoscopic examination. Clinical suspicion for idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH) prompted the recommendation for therapeutic lumbar puncture to alleviate symptoms and lower intracranial pressure.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH).

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH) with symptomatic relief.

Procedure:

The patient underwent therapeutic lumbar puncture with the goal of removing a volume of CSF to lower intracranial pressure. After sterile preparation and local anesthesia, CSF was slowly drained via the spinal needle, typically removing 20-30 ml of fluid or until the opening pressure was reduced.

Impression:

Therapeutic lumbar puncture resulted in a significant reduction in intracranial pressure, alleviating the patient's headaches and improving visual symptoms. Fundoscopic examination post-procedure demonstrated resolution of papilledema, indicating successful management of idiopathic intracranial hypertension.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was monitored closely for any complications related to the lumbar puncture, including post-lumbar puncture headache or leakage of CSF. Symptomatic relief and improvement in visual symptoms were observed, with plans for ongoing management of idiopathic intracranial hypertension, including weight management, medications, and regular ophthalmological follow-up to monitor for recurrence of symptoms.
