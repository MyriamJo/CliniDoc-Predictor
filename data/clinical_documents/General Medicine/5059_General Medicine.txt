Reason for Consultation:

A 28-year-old man presents with a history of ulcerative colitis flare-up and abdominal pain.

History of Present Illness:

The patient has a known history of ulcerative colitis, diagnosed 5 years ago, with intermittent flares managed with mesalamine and occasional corticosteroids. He reports experiencing an exacerbation of symptoms over the past week, characterized by increased abdominal pain, urgency, and frequency of bowel movements. He describes passing loose stools with blood and mucus, accompanied by tenesmus.

The patient denies any recent changes in diet, medication non-compliance, or known triggers for his ulcerative colitis flares.

Coronary Risk Factors:

The patient has no known coronary risk factors.

Family History:

Negative for coronary artery disease.

Medications:

1. Mesalamine for maintenance therapy of ulcerative colitis.
2. Occasional corticosteroids for ulcerative colitis flares.

Allergies:

None reported.

Personal History:

The patient denies smoking, alcohol consumption, or illicit drug use.

Past Medical History:

Significant for ulcerative colitis diagnosed 5 years ago.

Review of Systems:

Constitutional: Abdominal pain and urgency.
Gastrointestinal: Increased frequency of loose stools with blood and mucus, tenesmus.
Respiratory: No cough or shortness of breath.
Cardiovascular: No chest pain or palpitations.
Neurological: No focal weakness or sensory changes.
Psychological: No anxiety or depression.

Physical Examination:

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 120/80 mmHg, pulse 80 bpm, temperature 98.6°F.
General: Appears uncomfortable due to abdominal pain.
Abdominal: Mild tenderness on palpation over the lower abdomen, no rebound tenderness or guarding, bowel sounds present.
Rectal: Fecal occult blood test positive, no external hemorrhoids or anal fissures.

Assessment and Plan:

The patient's presentation is consistent with an exacerbation of ulcerative colitis, characterized by increased abdominal pain, urgency, and bloody diarrhea.

Immediate management will include optimization of mesalamine therapy and initiation of corticosteroids to induce remission. Assessment for potential triggers of the flare, such as dietary changes or stress, will be conducted.

The patient will be closely monitored for improvement in symptoms and signs of disease activity. Laboratory tests, including inflammatory markers and stool studies, will be ordered to assess disease severity and exclude infectious causes.

Patient education on ulcerative colitis management, including the importance of medication adherence and regular follow-up, will be provided. Close follow-up appointments will be scheduled to ensure resolution of the flare and to optimize long-term disease control.

Referral to a gastroenterologist may be considered for further evaluation and management if needed, including consideration of biologic therapy or surgical consultation.
