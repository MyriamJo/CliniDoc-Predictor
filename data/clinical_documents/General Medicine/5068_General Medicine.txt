Chief Complaint:

"I've been struggling with uncontrolled diabetes."

History of Present Illness:

A 62-year-old woman presents with concerns about her poorly controlled diabetes. She reports experiencing increasing difficulty in managing her blood sugar levels over the past several months despite adherence to her prescribed medications and dietary modifications. She describes frequent episodes of hyperglycemia, accompanied by symptoms of increased thirst, frequent urination, and fatigue. Additionally, she has noticed intermittent blurred vision and tingling sensations in her feet. Despite her efforts, she feels frustrated and overwhelmed by her inability to keep her blood sugar within target ranges.

Past Medical History:

The patient has a history of type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed 15 years ago, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia.

Family History:

Positive for diabetes mellitus and cardiovascular disease.

Social History:

The patient is a retired teacher. She lives alone and reports a sedentary lifestyle with limited physical activity. She denies smoking or alcohol consumption.

Medications:

Metformin 1000 mg twice daily, glimepiride 4 mg daily, lisinopril 10 mg daily, atorvastatin 20 mg daily.

Allergies:

No known drug allergies.

Objective:

General: The patient appears fatigued but is alert and oriented.

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 140/85 mmHg, pulse 78 beats per minute, respiratory rate 16 breaths per minute, temperature 98.6°F (37°C).

Physical Examination:

- Abdomen: Soft, non-tender, and non-distended. No hepatosplenomegaly appreciated.
- Neurological: No focal deficits. Distal sensation diminished in both feet. No motor weakness noted.
- Extremities: Bilateral pedal edema present up to the mid-shin level. No signs of peripheral artery disease.

Assessment:

The patient presents with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus with symptoms suggestive of hyperglycemia and peripheral neuropathy. Additionally, bilateral pedal edema raises concern for underlying diabetic nephropathy.

Plan:

Management plan includes:
- Review and optimization of current antidiabetic regimen, with consideration for intensification of therapy or addition of insulin.
- Dietary counseling and reinforcement of lifestyle modifications, including regular physical activity and weight management.
- Referral to an ophthalmologist for a comprehensive eye examination due to intermittent blurred vision.
- Evaluation of kidney function with serum creatinine and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio to assess for diabetic nephropathy.
- Education regarding the importance of regular blood glucose monitoring, medication adherence, and early recognition of symptoms of hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia.
- Follow-up appointment scheduled in two weeks for glycemic monitoring and further management adjustments. Referral to endocrinology may be considered for specialized care if needed.
