Chief Complaint:

"Fainted at school, feeling weak and dizzy."

History of Present Illness:

A 16-year-old adolescent presents to the emergency department after fainting at school. He reports feeling weak, dizzy, and lightheaded before losing consciousness. He was found by a teacher and brought to the nurse's office, where his blood sugar level was found to be significantly elevated. Prior to this episode, the patient had been experiencing increased thirst, frequent urination, and fatigue for several weeks. His symptoms had been dismissed as stress related to school exams by his parents. However, the fainting episode prompted further evaluation, leading to the discovery of diabetes.

Past Medical History:

No significant medical history reported prior to this episode.

Family History:

Negative for diabetes or other significant medical conditions.

Social History:

The patient is a high school student, actively involved in sports and extracurricular activities. He denies smoking, alcohol, or drug use.

Medications:

None reported.

Allergies:

No known drug allergies.

Objective:

General: The patient appears pale, weak, and fatigued but is conscious and responsive.

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 110/70 mmHg, pulse 90 beats per minute, respiratory rate 18 breaths per minute, temperature 98.2°F (36.8°C).

Physical Examination:

- Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs or abnormal sounds appreciated.
- Respiratory: Clear breath sounds bilaterally, no signs of respiratory distress.
- Abdomen: Soft, non-tender, and non-distended.
- Neurological: Alert and oriented, no focal deficits noted.

Assessment:

The patient presents with a history of syncope secondary to hyperglycemia, leading to the diagnosis of diabetes mellitus. He likely experienced hypoglycemia-induced syncope due to undiagnosed diabetes.

Plan:

Management plan includes:
- Immediate stabilization with intravenous fluids and glucose administration to correct hypoglycemia.
- Initiation of insulin therapy for glycemic control, along with education regarding insulin administration and blood glucose monitoring.
- Comprehensive diabetes education for the patient and family, including dietary modifications, exercise recommendations, and recognition of hypoglycemic and hyperglycemic symptoms.
- Referral to a pediatric endocrinologist for further evaluation and long-term management of diabetes.
- Counseling and support for the patient and family to address emotional and psychological aspects of living with a chronic condition.
- Follow-up appointment scheduled within one week for monitoring of blood glucose levels and adjustment of insulin therapy as needed. Continued support and education will be provided to ensure optimal diabetes management and prevent future complications.
