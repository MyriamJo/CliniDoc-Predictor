Chief Complaint:

"My child fainted suddenly, and we discovered he has diabetes."

History of Present Illness:

A concerned parent brings in their 15-year-old adolescent son, who recently fainted at home. The fainting episode occurred suddenly, without warning, and lasted for a few minutes before he regained consciousness. Upon awakening, he appeared confused and disoriented. The parents noted that he had been complaining of increased thirst, frequent urination, and fatigue over the past few weeks. They also observed a significant increase in his appetite coupled with unintentional weight loss. Concerned about these symptoms, they brought him to the clinic for evaluation.

Past Medical History:

The patient has no significant past medical history of diabetes or other chronic illnesses.

Family History:

Positive for type 1 diabetes mellitus in a maternal aunt.

Social History:

The patient is a high school student. He denies smoking or alcohol consumption. He is involved in regular physical activities, including sports.

Medications:

None.

Allergies:

No known drug allergies.

Objective:

General: The patient appears alert but fatigued.

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 110/70 mmHg, pulse 80 beats per minute, respiratory rate 16 breaths per minute, temperature 98.8°F (37.1°C).

Physical Examination:

- Neurological: Alert and oriented. No focal deficits. No signs of meningismus.
- Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm. No murmurs, rubs, or gallops.
- Respiratory: Clear lung fields bilaterally.
- Abdomen: Soft and non-tender. No hepatosplenomegaly appreciated.
- Extremities: No signs of peripheral edema. Capillary refill less than 2 seconds in all extremities.

Assessment:

The patient presents with a history of syncope and symptoms suggestive of hyperglycemia, including polyuria, polydipsia, increased appetite, and unintentional weight loss. Given the family history of type 1 diabetes mellitus and the clinical presentation, the possibility of new-onset diabetes mellitus, particularly type 1, is high.

Plan:

Management plan includes:
- Immediate referral to the emergency department for further evaluation and management, including confirmation of diabetes diagnosis through blood glucose and HbA1c testing.
- Initiation of insulin therapy as soon as the diagnosis is confirmed.
- Education of the patient and family regarding diabetes management, including blood glucose monitoring, insulin administration, carbohydrate counting, and recognition of hypoglycemic and hyperglycemic symptoms.
- Coordination with pediatric endocrinology for ongoing management and support.
- Counseling on lifestyle modifications, including dietary changes and regular physical activity, to optimize glycemic control and overall health.
- Psychological support for the patient and family to cope with the diagnosis and adjustment to the new treatment regimen.
- Follow-up appointment scheduled within a week for glycemic monitoring and further education reinforcement.
