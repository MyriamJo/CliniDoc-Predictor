Title of Procedure: 

Electromyography-Guided Trigger Point Injection for Myofascial Pain Syndrome

Indication for Procedure:

A 38-year-old female presented with chronic, localized muscle pain and tenderness in the right trapezius muscle, suggestive of myofascial pain syndrome. Clinical examination revealed trigger points with associated referred pain patterns. Electromyography (EMG) was recommended to confirm muscle involvement and guide targeted intervention.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Right trapezius myofascial pain syndrome.

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Right trapezius myofascial pain syndrome.

Procedure:

The patient was positioned comfortably in a seated position. Following skin preparation, a monopolar needle electrode was inserted into the trapezius muscle at the site of the trigger point. EMG was performed to confirm the presence of spontaneous electrical activity and characteristic motor unit action potentials associated with myofascial pain.

Under real-time EMG guidance, a mixture of local anesthetic and corticosteroid was injected directly into the trigger point to provide both immediate pain relief and long-term reduction in inflammation. The injection was performed using a fine-gauge needle to minimize patient discomfort and optimize medication delivery.

Impression:

EMG findings support the diagnosis of right trapezius myofascial pain syndrome. Trigger point injection performed under EMG guidance for targeted therapy.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was advised on post-injection care, including gentle stretching exercises and application of heat or ice packs as needed.

Follow-up:

Follow-up appointments were scheduled to monitor pain relief and assess the need for additional injections or adjunctive therapies.
