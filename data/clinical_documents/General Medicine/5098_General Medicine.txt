HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
A 45-year-old male presents with a one-week history of progressive shortness of breath and non-productive cough. He reports experiencing mild chest tightness, particularly with exertion, but denies any chest pain, wheezing, or orthopnea. He also complains of generalized weakness and fatigue. Symptoms started insidiously and have been progressively worsening. He denies any recent travel or exposure to sick contacts.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
History of hypertension, well-controlled on amlodipine.
No history of diabetes mellitus or cardiovascular disease.

SOCIAL HISTORY:
Works as an office manager. Non-smoker, occasional alcohol consumption.

FAMILY HISTORY:
Father had a myocardial infarction at age 50.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Vital Signs: Temperature 98.2°F, Blood Pressure 140/90 mmHg, Pulse 90 bpm, Respiratory Rate 20 breaths/min.
Respiratory: Diminished breath sounds at lung bases bilaterally. No wheezing or crackles appreciated.
Cardiovascular: Regular rhythm without murmurs.
Abdominal: Soft and non-tender.

ASSESSMENT:

Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD): Suspected diagnosis based on clinical presentation and examination findings.
Hypertension: History of hypertension requiring ongoing management.
PLAN:

Pulmonary Function Tests: Order spirometry to confirm the diagnosis and assess severity of airflow limitation.
Smoking Cessation Counseling: Provide counseling and resources for smoking cessation to reduce disease progression.
Bronchodilator Therapy: Initiate bronchodilator therapy with short-acting beta-agonists and consider long-acting bronchodilators for maintenance.
Blood Pressure Management: Optimize blood pressure control with antihypertensive medication adjustment as needed.
