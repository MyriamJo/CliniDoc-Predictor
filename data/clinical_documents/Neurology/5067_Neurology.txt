Title of Procedure: Prolonged Electroencephalogram (EEG) Monitoring for Evaluation of Altered Mental Status

Indication for Procedure:

A 60-year-old male presented with episodes of confusion, disorientation, and memory impairment. Clinical examination revealed no focal neurological deficits, but cognitive testing suggested impairment. Prolonged electroencephalogram (EEG) monitoring was recommended to evaluate for underlying epileptiform activity or other cerebral dysfunction.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Altered mental status.

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Altered mental status.

Procedure:

The patient was positioned comfortably in a reclined chair or bed in a quiet, monitored environment. Electrodes were applied to the scalp according to the international 10-20 system, with additional electrodes placed to capture extended periods of EEG recording.

Continuous EEG monitoring was initiated using digital recording equipment with video monitoring capabilities. The patient's mental status and behavior were observed throughout the recording period. EEG technologists remained available to address any technical issues or patient concerns.

Impression:

Prolonged EEG findings demonstrate intermittent slowing and epileptiform discharges consistent with a diagnosis of non-convulsive status epilepticus. Electroencephalogram monitoring performed for evaluation of altered mental status.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was closely monitored post-procedure for any adverse events or changes in mental status. Results from the prolonged EEG monitoring were interpreted by a neurologist or epileptologist to guide further diagnostic evaluation and treatment. Follow-up appointments were scheduled to discuss the findings and adjust management strategies as needed.
