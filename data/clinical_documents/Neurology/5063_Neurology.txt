Title of Procedure: 
Electromyography-Guided Peripheral Nerve Decompression Surgery for Chronic Compression Neuropathy

Indication for Procedure:

A 40-year-old female presented with chronic numbness and tingling in the left hand, suggestive of carpal tunnel syndrome. Clinical examination revealed positive Tinel's sign and Phalen's maneuver. Electromyography (EMG) was recommended to confirm nerve compression and guide targeted surgical intervention.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Left carpal tunnel syndrome.

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Left carpal tunnel syndrome.

Procedure:

The patient was positioned comfortably in a supine position. Following skin preparation, needle electrodes were inserted along the distribution of the affected nerve to assess its electrical activity and degree of dysfunction.

Under real-time EMG guidance, surgical decompression of the median nerve was performed. The transverse carpal ligament was released to relieve pressure on the median nerve. Intraoperative EMG monitoring was utilized to ensure adequate decompression while preserving nerve function.

Impression:

EMG findings support the diagnosis of left carpal tunnel syndrome. Electromyography-guided peripheral nerve decompression surgery performed for targeted relief of nerve compression.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was instructed on postoperative care, including hand elevation, wound care, and activity modification. Occupational therapy was initiated to facilitate hand rehabilitation and prevent postoperative stiffness. Follow-up appointments were scheduled to monitor symptom improvement and assess functional outcomes.
