Medical Record:

Procedure: Gallbladder Ultrasound (Sonography)

Reason for Procedure:

Intermittent upper abdominal discomfort, particularly after meals, with associated bloating and belching, suggestive of possible biliary dyskinesia.

Ultrasound Findings:

The gallbladder ultrasound was performed to evaluate the patient's symptoms. The examination revealed the following findings:

1. Gallbladder Sludge: Thickened bile with particulate matter, commonly referred to as gallbladder sludge, was observed within the gallbladder lumen. This sludge may contribute to symptoms of biliary dyskinesia.

2. Normal Gallbladder Wall Thickness: The gallbladder wall appeared within normal limits, measuring approximately 3 millimeters, without evidence of inflammation or thickening.

3. No Gallstones: No discrete gallstones were visualized within the gallbladder lumen or cystic duct.

Impression:

The gallbladder ultrasound findings are consistent with gallbladder sludge, which may contribute to symptoms of biliary dyskinesia. However, the absence of gallstones and normal gallbladder wall thickness suggests that acute inflammation or obstruction is unlikely.

Recommendations:

1. Functional Testing: Consideration of further evaluation with a hepatobiliary iminodiacetic acid (HIDA) scan or cholescintigraphy to assess gallbladder function and rule out biliary dyskinesia.

2. Symptomatic Management: In the interim, symptomatic relief with dietary modifications (low-fat diet), antispasmodic medications, and lifestyle changes (e.g., stress reduction, small frequent meals) may be recommended to alleviate symptoms of biliary dyskinesia.

3. Follow-up: The patient is advised to follow up with their healthcare provider for discussion of test results and formulation of an appropriate management plan tailored to their symptoms.

Follow-up:

The patient is scheduled for a follow-up appointment to discuss the results of the gallbladder ultrasound and further management options for symptomatic relief. Depending on the clinical course, additional investigations or interventions may be considered.
