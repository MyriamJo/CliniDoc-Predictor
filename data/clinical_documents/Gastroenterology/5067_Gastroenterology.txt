Medical Record:

Procedure: Cholecystectomy (Gallbladder Removal)

Reason for Procedure:

Chronic gallstone disease with recurrent episodes of biliary colic and suspicion of acute cholecystitis.

Operative Details:

The patient underwent cholecystectomy, a surgical procedure to remove the gallbladder, under general anesthesia. The operation was performed using laparoscopic techniques, involving several small incisions in the abdomen for insertion of specialized instruments and a camera (laparoscope).

The surgeon carefully dissected and freed the gallbladder from surrounding tissues, ensuring to identify and preserve vital structures such as the cystic duct and artery. The cystic duct, which connects the gallbladder to the common bile duct, was ligated and divided. Following this, the gallbladder was safely removed from the abdomen through one of the small incisions.

Hemostasis was meticulously achieved, and the operative site was inspected for any signs of bleeding or bile leakage. The incisions were closed with sutures or surgical staples, and a sterile dressing was applied.

Impression:

Successful laparoscopic cholecystectomy performed without intraoperative complications. The gallbladder was removed due to chronic gallstone disease, aiming to alleviate symptoms and prevent future episodes of biliary colic or acute cholecystitis.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was monitored closely in the recovery area for any signs of postoperative complications such as bleeding, infection, or bile duct injury. Pain management, fluid intake, and early mobilization were initiated to facilitate recovery.

Dietary instructions were provided, emphasizing a gradual return to a normal diet, avoiding fatty or greasy foods initially to prevent digestive discomfort.

Follow-up:

The patient was scheduled for a follow-up appointment with the surgeon to assess postoperative recovery, review pathology results, and discuss any concerns or questions regarding the procedure. Long-term follow-up may involve monitoring for potential complications such as bile duct stones or post-cholecystectomy syndrome.
