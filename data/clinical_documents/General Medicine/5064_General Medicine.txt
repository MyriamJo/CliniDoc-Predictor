Subjective:

The patient, a 45-year-old woman, presents with complaints of persistent tingling and numbness in her hands and feet, along with weakness and fatigue. She reports that the symptoms started gradually about six months ago and have been progressively worsening. The tingling and numbness are most prominent in the fingertips and toes, and she describes difficulty with fine motor tasks and walking long distances. The patient denies any recent injuries or trauma to her hands or feet.

Past Medical History:

The patient has a history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, diagnosed five years ago, which is currently managed with oral medications.

Family History:

Positive for diabetes mellitus in her mother and maternal aunt.

Objective:

General: The patient appears fatigued and slightly overweight but is otherwise well-groomed and cooperative.

Neurological Examination: Motor strength is diminished in the distal muscles of the hands and feet, with decreased grip strength and difficulty with toe and heel walking. Sensation to light touch is reduced in a stocking-glove distribution. Deep tendon reflexes are diminished, and there is evidence of bilateral foot drop.

Musculoskeletal Examination: No signs of joint deformities or swelling noted.

Skin Examination: No abnormalities or lesions observed on the skin.

Assessment:

The patient is suspected to be experiencing peripheral neuropathy secondary to diabetic neuropathy, given her history of diabetes mellitus and the characteristic distribution of symptoms.

Plan:

Management plan includes:
- Optimization of diabetes management through lifestyle modifications, including diet and exercise, and adjustment of medication regimen as needed.
- Symptomatic relief with neuropathic pain medications such as gabapentin or pregabalin to alleviate tingling and numbness.
- Physical therapy referral for strengthening exercises and gait training to address weakness and improve mobility.
- Regular monitoring of blood glucose levels and diabetic neuropathy symptoms to assess response to treatment and prevent further complications. Referral to neurology for further evaluation and management may be considered if symptoms persist or worsen despite initial management.
