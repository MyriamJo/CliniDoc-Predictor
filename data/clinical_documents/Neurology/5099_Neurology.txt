Title of Procedure:
Lumbar Puncture for Evaluation of Multiple Sclerosis

Indication for Procedure:

A 30-year-old male presented with recurrent episodes of visual disturbances, sensory deficits, and gait instability. Clinical examination revealed bilateral optic neuritis and hyperreflexia. Suspected diagnosis of multiple sclerosis (MS) prompted the recommendation for a lumbar puncture to assess cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) for the presence of oligoclonal bands and immunoglobulin G (IgG) index.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Suspected multiple sclerosis (MS).

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Multiple sclerosis (MS).

Procedure:

The patient was positioned in the lateral decubitus position, and the lumbar region was prepped and draped in a sterile manner. After local anesthesia, a spinal needle was inserted into the subarachnoid space, and CSF samples were collected and sent for analysis. CSF was evaluated for oligoclonal bands and IgG index.

Impression:

Lumbar puncture revealed the presence of oligoclonal bands and elevated IgG index in the cerebrospinal fluid, consistent with a diagnosis of multiple sclerosis. The findings supported the diagnosis of MS as the underlying cause of the patient's clinical symptoms, including optic neuritis and sensory deficits.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was referred to a neurologist specializing in multiple sclerosis for further evaluation and management. Treatment options, including disease-modifying therapies such as corticosteroids or immunomodulatory agents, were discussed based on the lumbar puncture findings and disease severity. Close monitoring for disease progression and response to treatment was initiated, with plans for regular follow-up appointments and imaging studies to assess disease activity.
