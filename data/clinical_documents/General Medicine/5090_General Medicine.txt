HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
A 7-year-old male presents with recurrent fevers over the past month. His mother reports episodes of fever spikes up to 102°F, accompanied by chills and generalized malaise. The fever typically resolves spontaneously after a few days, only to recur again. The patient denies any sore throat, cough, or rash. He has experienced decreased appetite but continues to drink fluids adequately. No recent travel or sick contacts reported.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
History of recurrent tonsillitis, with the last episode occurring six months ago.

PAST SURGICAL HISTORY:
Tonsillectomy at age 5 due to recurrent tonsillitis.

FAMILY HISTORY:
Negative for any significant medical conditions.

SOCIAL HISTORY:
Attends elementary school, lives with parents and two siblings. No exposure to tobacco smoke or pets at home.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Vital Signs: Temperature 101.5°F, other vital signs within normal limits.
General: Alert and oriented, no acute distress.
Head and Neck: Tonsillectomy scars noted, oropharynx clear.
Cardiovascular: Regular heart rate and rhythm, no murmurs.
Respiratory: Clear breath sounds bilaterally, no wheezing or crackles.
Abdomen: Soft and non-tender, no organomegaly appreciated.
Skin: No rashes or lesions noted.

ASSESSMENT:

Recurrent Fevers: Suspected infectious etiology given the recurrent nature of fevers and absence of localizing symptoms. Differential diagnosis includes viral infections, occult bacteremia, or atypical presentations of bacterial infections.
PLAN:

Diagnostic Workup: Order complete blood count, blood cultures, and urinalysis to evaluate for possible underlying infection.
Symptomatic Management: Recommend antipyretics for fever control and adequate fluid intake to prevent dehydration.
Follow-up: Schedule a follow-up appointment in one week to review laboratory results and assess for resolution of symptoms.
