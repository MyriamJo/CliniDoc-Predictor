Title of Procedure: Electromyography-Guided Botulinum Toxin Injection for Chronic Migraine

Indication for Procedure:

A 35-year-old male presented with chronic migraine headaches refractory to conventional treatments. Clinical examination revealed trigger points and muscle tenderness in the cervical and temporal regions. Electromyography (EMG) was recommended to assess muscle involvement and guide targeted treatment.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Chronic migraine with associated muscle tension.

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Chronic migraine with associated muscle tension.

Procedure:

The patient was positioned comfortably in a seated position. Following skin preparation, needle electrodes were inserted into the affected muscles, including the trapezius and temporalis muscles, to assess their electrical activity and degree of muscle tension.

Under real-time EMG guidance, botulinum toxin was injected into the overactive muscles to induce localized chemical denervation and reduce muscle tension. Multiple injection sites were targeted based on EMG findings, with careful consideration given to muscle size and distribution of tension.

Impression:

EMG findings support the diagnosis of chronic migraine with associated muscle tension. Electromyography-guided botulinum toxin injection performed for targeted muscle relaxation.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was monitored for immediate side effects, such as temporary weakness or injection site discomfort. A comprehensive migraine management plan, including lifestyle modifications and pharmacological treatments, was discussed. Follow-up appointments were scheduled to assess treatment response and plan for further interventions as needed.
