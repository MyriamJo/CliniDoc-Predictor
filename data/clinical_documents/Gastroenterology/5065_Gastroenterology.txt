Medical Record:

Procedure: Gallbladder Ultrasound (Sonography)

Reason for Procedure:

Episodes of severe right upper quadrant abdominal pain, accompanied by fever and jaundice, suggestive of acute cholecystitis or choledocholithiasis.

Ultrasound Findings:

The gallbladder ultrasound was performed urgently to assess the patient's symptoms. The examination revealed the following findings:

1. Gallstones: Numerous hyperechoic structures were visualized within the gallbladder lumen, consistent with multiple gallstones of varying sizes. Some stones measured up to 2 centimeters in diameter.

2. Gallbladder Wall Thickening: The gallbladder wall appeared markedly thickened, measuring approximately 8 millimeters, indicative of acute inflammation and edema, supporting the diagnosis of acute cholecystitis.

3. Common Bile Duct (CBD) Dilatation: Dilatation of the common bile duct was noted, measuring up to 1.5 centimeters in diameter, raising concerns for possible choledocholithiasis or obstruction.

Impression:

The gallbladder ultrasound findings are highly suggestive of acute cholecystitis with associated gallstones and possible choledocholithiasis. The presence of gallstones, gallbladder wall thickening, and CBD dilatation indicate an urgent need for further evaluation and intervention.

Recommendations:

1. Immediate Medical Intervention: Urgent consultation with a gastroenterologist or general surgeon for further evaluation and management of acute cholecystitis and possible choledocholithiasis. Antibiotics, analgesics, and intravenous fluids may be initiated to stabilize the patient and manage the infection.

2. Consideration for Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) or Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography (MRCP): Further imaging studies such as ERCP or MRCP may be indicated to evaluate the CBD for the presence of stones or other obstructive lesions.

3. Surgical Consultation: Discussion of the possibility of cholecystectomy and/or CBD stone extraction to address the underlying pathology and prevent complications.

Follow-up:

The patient requires immediate admission to the hospital for further evaluation and management of acute cholecystitis and possible choledocholithiasis. Timely intervention is essential to prevent complications such as gallbladder perforation, bile duct obstruction, or ascending cholangitis.
