Reason for Visit:

This 36-year-old man presents with complaints of persistent headaches and difficulty concentrating at work.

History of Present Illness:

The patient reports a recent onset of headaches that have been progressively worsening over the past few weeks. He describes the headaches as a dull pressure mainly concentrated in the frontal region, often accompanied by a sensation of heaviness behind the eyes. The headaches typically start in the late afternoon and persist until bedtime, sometimes interfering with his ability to fall asleep. He also notes difficulty concentrating at work, finding himself easily distracted and unable to focus on tasks for prolonged periods. Additionally, he has experienced episodes of irritability and mood swings, which he attributes to his inability to get adequate rest.

The patient's sleep patterns have been disrupted lately, with difficulty falling asleep and frequent awakenings throughout the night. He reports feeling restless during sleep, tossing and turning in bed. Despite spending sufficient time in bed, he wakes up feeling unrefreshed and fatigued in the morning. He denies any history of snoring or witnessed apneic episodes during sleep.

During the daytime, the patient admits to consuming multiple cups of coffee to combat fatigue and stay alert. He also indulges in sugary snacks and energy drinks to boost his energy levels. Despite these efforts, he feels persistently tired and lacks motivation to engage in physical activities.

The patient has a sedentary lifestyle, spending most of his time sitting at his desk for work or watching television at home. He admits to smoking cigarettes occasionally, particularly during times of stress.

Past Medical History:

1. History of migraines.
2. Allergic rhinitis.
3. Chronic sinusitis.

Family History:

Positive for migraines and mood disorders.

Social History:

The patient works in a high-stress environment and lives alone. He denies any history of substance abuse.

Review of Systems:

Not contributory.

Medications:

The patient takes the following medications:
1. Ibuprofen as needed for headaches.
2. Loratadine for allergic rhinitis.
3. Nasal decongestant spray as needed.

Findings:

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 130/80, pulse 72, respiratory rate 16, weight 180 pounds, height 6 feet, temperature 98.6 degrees Fahrenheit. HEENT: Normocephalic and atraumatic. Pupils equal, round, and reactive to light. Nasal passages clear with mild mucosal edema. Oropharynx without erythema or exudates. Neck: Supple without lymphadenopathy or masses. Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs. Lungs: Clear to auscultation bilaterally. Abdomen: Soft, nontender, nondistended. Extremities: No edema, normal range of motion.

Laboratory and Imaging:

No abnormalities noted on routine blood work.

Assessment and Plan:

The patient's symptoms are suggestive of a multifactorial etiology, including migraines, sleep disruption, and caffeine dependence.

Regarding the headaches, I will initiate a trial of a migraine prophylactic medication and advise the patient to keep a headache diary to identify triggers. Lifestyle modifications, including stress reduction techniques and regular exercise, will also be encouraged.

For the sleep disturbances, I will recommend implementing sleep hygiene practices such as maintaining a consistent sleep schedule, creating a relaxing bedtime routine, and avoiding caffeine and electronics before bedtime. Referral to a sleep specialist may be considered if symptoms persist.

To address the patient's dependence on caffeine, I will advise gradual reduction of caffeine intake and substitute healthier alternatives such as herbal tea or water. Additionally, counseling on stress management techniques will be provided to help alleviate work-related stressors.

Follow-up appointments will be scheduled to monitor the patient's response to treatment and make any necessary adjustments.
