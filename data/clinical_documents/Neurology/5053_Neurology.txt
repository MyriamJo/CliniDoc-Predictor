Title of Procedure:
Electromyography-Guided Botulinum Toxin Injection for Cervical Dystonia

Indication for Procedure:

A 50-year-old male presented with progressive neck muscle spasms and abnormal head posture consistent with cervical dystonia. Clinical examination revealed sustained muscle contractions, causing involuntary head rotation and tilting. Electromyography (EMG) was recommended to confirm muscle involvement and guide targeted treatment with botulinum toxin.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Cervical dystonia.

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Cervical dystonia.

Procedure:

The patient was positioned comfortably in a seated position. Following skin preparation, needle electrodes were inserted into specific neck muscles affected by dystonia, including the sternocleidomastoid and trapezius muscles. EMG was performed to confirm abnormal muscle activity and identify optimal injection sites.

Under real-time EMG guidance, botulinum toxin was injected into the affected muscles to induce localized chemical denervation and reduce muscle hyperactivity. Multiple injection sites were targeted based on EMG findings, with careful consideration given to muscle size and distribution of dystonic movements.

Impression:

EMG findings support the diagnosis of cervical dystonia. Botulinum toxin injection performed under EMG guidance for targeted therapy.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was monitored for immediate side effects, such as injection site discomfort or transient weakness. Post-injection instructions were provided, including recommendations for maintaining proper head and neck alignment and avoiding strenuous activities.

Follow-up:

Follow-up appointments were scheduled to assess treatment response and plan for subsequent injections as needed.
