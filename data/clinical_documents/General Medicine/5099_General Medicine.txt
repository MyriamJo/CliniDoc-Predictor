HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
A 30-year-old female presents with a two-day history of progressively worsening right lower quadrant abdominal pain. She describes the pain as sharp and constant, exacerbated by movement and deep inspiration. She also reports associated nausea and decreased appetite. She denies any fever, vomiting, or changes in bowel habits.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
Non-contributory.

SOCIAL HISTORY:
Works as a graphic designer. Non-smoker, occasional alcohol consumption.

FAMILY HISTORY:
Non-contributory.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Vital Signs: Temperature 98.6°F, Blood Pressure 110/70 mmHg, Pulse 80 bpm, Respiratory Rate 16 breaths/min.
Abdominal: Tenderness and guarding localized to the right lower quadrant, with rebound tenderness and positive McBurney's sign.
Gynecological: Normal external genitalia, no cervical motion tenderness.

ASSESSMENT:

Acute Appendicitis: Suspected diagnosis based on clinical presentation and examination findings.
Differential Diagnosis: Consideration for other causes of right lower quadrant pain, including ovarian cyst rupture or pelvic inflammatory disease.
PLAN:

Abdominal Imaging: Order abdominal ultrasound or CT scan for confirmation of appendicitis and evaluation of other intra-abdominal pathology.
Surgical Consultation: Arrange for urgent surgical assessment and consideration for appendectomy.
Pain Management: Provide analgesia to alleviate abdominal pain and discomfort.
NPO Status: Advise the patient to remain NPO (nil per os) pending further evaluation and surgical consultation.
