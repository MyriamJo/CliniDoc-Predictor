Title of Procedure:
Ambulatory Electroencephalogram (EEG) Monitoring for Evaluation of Suspected Non-Epileptic Seizures

Indication for Procedure:

A 30-year-old female presented with episodes of altered consciousness and motor symptoms resembling seizures. However, diagnostic workup, including routine EEG, did not reveal epileptiform activity. Clinical suspicion for non-epileptic seizures was raised. Ambulatory EEG monitoring was recommended to capture and characterize these episodes outside of the hospital setting.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Suspected non-epileptic seizures.

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Suspected non-epileptic seizures.

Procedure:

The patient underwent placement of scalp electrodes according to the international 10-20 system, with additional electrodes for prolonged monitoring. A portable EEG recording device was attached, allowing continuous recording over 24 to 72 hours. The patient was instructed to go about their normal activities while wearing the equipment.

Continuous EEG monitoring was performed, capturing brain activity during various activities and potential seizure events. The patient was provided with a diary to document any symptoms experienced during the monitoring period, helping to correlate clinical events with EEG findings.

Impression:

Ambulatory EEG monitoring captured multiple episodes of altered consciousness and motor symptoms consistent with non-epileptic seizures. EEG findings did not demonstrate epileptiform activity during these events, supporting the diagnosis of non-epileptic seizures.

Postoperative Care:

The patient returned the ambulatory EEG equipment to the clinic for data analysis. Results were reviewed by a neurologist specializing in epilepsy and seizure disorders, who interpreted the findings and discussed the implications with the patient. Counseling and education regarding non-epileptic seizures were provided, and further management strategies were discussed during follow-up appointments.
