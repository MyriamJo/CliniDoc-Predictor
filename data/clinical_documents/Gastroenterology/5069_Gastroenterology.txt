Medical Record:

Procedure: Cholecystectomy (Gallbladder Removal)

Reason for Procedure:

Chronic and debilitating upper abdominal pain, especially after meals, accompanied by bloating and indigestion, suggestive of gallstone-related biliary colic.

Operative Details:

The patient underwent cholecystectomy, a surgical procedure to remove the gallbladder, performed under general anesthesia. The operation was carried out using a laparoscopic approach to minimize surgical trauma and enhance recovery.

During the procedure, small incisions were made in the abdomen to introduce specialized instruments and a laparoscope for visualization. The surgeon meticulously dissected the gallbladder from its attachments to the liver and surrounding tissues, taking care to identify and preserve vital structures such as the cystic duct and artery.

Once the gallbladder was completely mobilized, the cystic duct and artery were ligated and divided, allowing for safe removal of the gallbladder. The surgical site was inspected for any signs of bleeding or bile leakage, and appropriate hemostasis was achieved before closing the incisions with sutures or surgical staples.

Impression:

Successful laparoscopic cholecystectomy performed without intraoperative complications. The procedure was indicated due to chronic biliary colic attributed to gallstone disease, aiming to alleviate symptoms and improve quality of life.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was closely monitored in the post-anesthesia care unit for any signs of postoperative complications such as pain, bleeding, or infection. Pain management, intravenous fluids, and anti-nausea medications were administered as needed to ensure comfort and recovery.

Instructions were provided for postoperative care, including dietary modifications to avoid fatty or greasy foods initially and gradually reintroduce regular diet as tolerated. The patient was advised to avoid heavy lifting or strenuous activity during the initial recovery period.

Follow-up:

The patient was scheduled for a follow-up appointment with the surgical team to assess postoperative progress, review pathology results, and address any concerns or questions regarding the procedure. Long-term follow-up may involve monitoring for potential complications such as bile duct injury or development of post-cholecystectomy syndrome.
