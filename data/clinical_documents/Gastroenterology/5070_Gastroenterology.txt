Procedure: Cholecystectomy (Gallbladder Removal)

Reason for Procedure:

The patient presented with recurrent right upper quadrant abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting, consistent with symptomatic gallstone disease.

Operative Details:

Under general anesthesia, a laparoscopic cholecystectomy was performed. The surgeon made small incisions in the abdomen to access and remove the gallbladder. Careful dissection was carried out to preserve surrounding structures, including the cystic duct and artery. The gallbladder was then extracted using specialized instruments.

Impression:

Successful laparoscopic cholecystectomy was completed to address symptomatic gallstone disease.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was monitored closely in the recovery area for any signs of postoperative complications such as bleeding, infection, or bile leakage. Pain management and intravenous fluids were administered as needed to ensure comfort and hydration.

Instructions were provided for postoperative care, including gradual advancement of diet and avoidance of heavy lifting or strenuous activity during the initial recovery period.

Follow-up:

The patient was scheduled for a follow-up appointment with the surgical team to assess postoperative progress, review pathology results, and address any concerns or questions regarding the procedure. Long-term follow-up may involve monitoring for potential complications such as bile duct injury or development of post-cholecystectomy syndrome.
