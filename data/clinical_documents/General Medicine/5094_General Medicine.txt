HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
An 80-year-old female presents with a two-month history of persistent sadness, decreased interest in activities, insomnia, and feelings of worthlessness. She reports a loss of appetite and unintentional weight loss over the same period. The patient denies any suicidal ideation but acknowledges feelings of hopelessness and despair. Symptoms have progressively worsened, affecting her ability to perform daily tasks and engage in social interactions.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
History of hypertension, managed with antihypertensive medications.

PAST SURGICAL HISTORY:
Cholecystectomy performed 10 years ago.

FAMILY HISTORY:
Positive for depression in her mother and sister.

SOCIAL HISTORY:
Widowed, lives alone, retired schoolteacher. No history of substance abuse.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Vital Signs: Within normal limits.
General: Appears sad and withdrawn, flat affect.
Neurological: No focal deficits, orientation intact.
Mood and Affect: Depressed mood, constricted affect.
Cognitive Function: Mild forgetfulness, intact attention and concentration.

ASSESSMENT:

Major Depressive Disorder:
Suspected diagnosis based on the patient's clinical presentation of persistent depressive symptoms lasting more than two months, with significant impairment in functioning.

PLAN:

Psychiatric Evaluation: 
Refer to a psychiatrist for comprehensive assessment and management of depression, including consideration for pharmacotherapy and psychotherapy.

Safety Assessment:
Evaluate for any suicidal ideation or intent, implement appropriate safety measures if indicated.

Supportive Care: 
Encourage social support from family and friends, engage in activities that provide a sense of pleasure and accomplishment.

Follow-up: 
Schedule a follow-up appointment in two weeks to monitor response to treatment and adjust management plan as necessary.
