Reason for Consultation:

A 30-year-old woman presents with a history of asthma exacerbation and shortness of breath.

History of Present Illness:

The patient reports a history of asthma diagnosed in childhood, with intermittent symptoms controlled by albuterol inhaler as needed. However, she experienced an acute worsening of symptoms over the past 2 days, characterized by increased shortness of breath, chest tightness, and wheezing. She describes difficulty performing her usual activities due to the severity of her symptoms.

The patient denies any recent upper respiratory tract infections, fevers, or exposure to known allergens or irritants.

Coronary Risk Factors:

The patient has no known coronary risk factors.

Family History:

Negative for coronary artery disease.

Medications:

1. Albuterol inhaler for asthma symptoms.

Allergies:

None reported.

Personal History:

The patient has no history of smoking or illicit drug use. She occasionally drinks alcohol socially.

Past Medical History:

Significant for childhood asthma.

Review of Systems:

Constitutional: Shortness of breath.
Respiratory: Increased dyspnea, wheezing.
Cardiovascular: No chest pain or palpitations.
Gastrointestinal: No gastrointestinal symptoms.
Neurological: No focal weakness or sensory changes.
Psychological: No anxiety or depression.

Physical Examination:

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 120/80 mmHg, pulse 100 bpm, respiratory rate 24/min, temperature 98.6°F.
General: Appears anxious, using accessory respiratory muscles.
Respiratory: Audible wheezing heard bilaterally, decreased air entry, prolonged expiratory phase.
Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs.
Abdomen: Soft, nontender, nondistended.
Extremities: No cyanosis or clubbing, peripheral pulses intact.

Assessment and Plan:

The patient's presentation is consistent with an acute exacerbation of asthma, characterized by worsening dyspnea, chest tightness, and wheezing.

Immediate management will include administration of inhaled bronchodilators (albuterol) and systemic corticosteroids to relieve bronchospasm and reduce airway inflammation. Oxygen therapy will be provided as needed to maintain adequate oxygen saturation.

Assessment for potential triggers of the exacerbation, such as allergen exposure or non-compliance with asthma medications, will be conducted. Patient education on asthma triggers and proper inhaler technique will be reinforced.

The patient will be closely monitored for improvement in symptoms and signs of respiratory distress. Close follow-up appointments will be scheduled to ensure resolution of the exacerbation and to optimize long-term asthma management.

Asthma action plan will be provided to the patient, outlining steps for managing future exacerbations and when to seek medical attention. Referral to an asthma specialist may be considered for further evaluation and management if needed.
