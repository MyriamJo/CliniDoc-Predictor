History of Present Illness:

A 49-year-old woman with a history of polyarteritis nodosa, mononeuritis multiplex involving the lower extremities, and severe sleep apnea presents for follow-up following an overnight sleep study. The patient reports ongoing difficulty in initiating and maintaining sleep despite being on continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) and oxygen therapy. She describes poor sleep quality, characterized by tossing and turning throughout the night and frequent mask removal. Additionally, she continues to experience significant leg pain from mononeuritis multiplex and lower extremity edema requiring Lasix therapy. Over the past few weeks, she has noticed a ten-pound weight gain and worsening fatigue.

Medications:

The patient's current medications include prednisone, Ativan, imipramine, MS Contin, immediate-release morphine, and Lasix. Prednisone dosage has been reduced, and she continues to use opiates for pain control.

Findings:

Vital signs show an elevated blood pressure, weight gain, and hypoxemia at rest. Physical examination reveals lower extremity pretibial edema with erythema.

Laboratory and Diagnostic Findings:

Arterial blood gas analysis demonstrates a compensated respiratory acidosis. CPAP compliance monitoring shows inadequate usage, with evidence of severe central sleep apnea on the overnight sleep study.

Assessment:

1. Obesity hypoventilation syndrome exacerbated by severe central sleep apnea and opiate use.
2. Chronic pain from mononeuritis multiplex necessitating high doses of analgesics.
3. Hypoxemia likely due to obesity, hypoventilation, and pulmonary pathology.
4. Increased lower extremity edema secondary to polyarteritis nodosa and Lasix therapy.

Plan:

1. Optimization of pain management by reducing evening doses of opiates.
2. Initiation of Automated Servo Ventilation (ASV) to address central sleep apnea.
3. Modification of Lasix therapy to promote diuresis and improve oxygenation.
4. Further evaluation with pulmonary function studies and high-resolution CT scanning to assess for underlying lung pathology contributing to hypoxemia.
5. Encouragement of modest weight loss measures to alleviate obesity-related respiratory complications.

Follow-up:

The patient will return for a trial of ASV therapy and additional assessments to monitor treatment response and adjust management strategies accordingly. Close monitoring of symptoms, medication adherence, and lifestyle modifications will be emphasized to optimize overall health and quality of life.
