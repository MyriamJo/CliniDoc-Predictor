Chief Complaint:
The patient presents with a persistent cough and shortness of breath.

History of Present Illness:
A 17-year-old male reports experiencing a dry cough and intermittent shortness of breath for the past two weeks. He denies any fever, chest pain, or sputum production. The cough tends to worsen at night and with physical exertion, such as during sports activities. He has no significant medical history or recent illnesses. He denies exposure to sick contacts or environmental allergens.

Past Medical History:
No significant past medical history reported.

Family History:
Negative for respiratory disorders.

Social History:
The patient is an active teenager, participating in regular sports activities and maintaining a healthy lifestyle. He denies smoking or illicit drug use.

Review of Systems:
Negative for fever, chills, night sweats, or weight loss. No history of gastrointestinal symptoms.

Physical Examination:
Vital Signs: Within normal limits.
Respiratory: Clear lung fields bilaterally. No wheezing or crackles appreciated.

Assessment:

Acute Cough: Likely due to a viral upper respiratory tract infection or exercise-induced bronchoconstriction.
Asthma: Consideration given to underlying asthma as a potential cause of symptoms.
Plan:

Symptomatic Treatment: Recommend supportive measures, including hydration, rest, and over-the-counter cough suppressants as needed.
Evaluation: Consider pulmonary function testing if symptoms persist beyond two weeks or worsen over time.
Follow-up: Schedule a follow-up appointment in two weeks for reassessment and further management as indicated.
