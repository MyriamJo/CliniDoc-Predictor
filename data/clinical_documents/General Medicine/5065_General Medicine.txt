Subjective:

The patient, a 45-year-old man, presents with complaints of persistent fatigue and yellowing of the skin and eyes. He reports feeling increasingly tired over the past month, with associated loss of appetite and intermittent nausea. Additionally, he has noticed a yellowish discoloration of his skin and eyes, which has been progressively worsening. The patient denies any significant abdominal pain, fever, or recent travel to areas endemic for viral hepatitis.

Past Medical History:

The patient has no significant past medical history of liver disease or hepatitis.

Family History:

Negative for significant liver disease or hepatitis.

Objective:

General: The patient appears fatigued and jaundiced but is alert and oriented.

Skin Examination: Jaundice noted, with yellow discoloration of the sclerae, skin, and mucous membranes. No signs of pruritus or rash observed.

Abdominal Examination: Soft and nontender abdomen, with no palpable hepatomegaly or splenomegaly appreciated. Liver edge may be palpable below the right costal margin.

Assessment:

The patient is suspected to have acute hepatitis based on the clinical presentation of fatigue, jaundice, and associated symptoms.

Plan:

Management plan includes:
- Laboratory investigations, including liver function tests (LFTs), hepatitis panel, and serological markers for viral hepatitis (e.g., hepatitis A, B, C).
- Symptomatic relief with adequate rest, hydration, and nutritional support.
- Advising the patient to avoid alcohol and hepatotoxic medications until the cause of hepatitis is determined.
- Referral to gastroenterology for further evaluation and management, including consideration for liver biopsy if indicated.
- Education on the importance of practicing safe sex and avoiding sharing needles to prevent transmission of viral hepatitis.
- Follow-up appointment scheduled in one week to review laboratory results and adjust management accordingly. Referral to hepatology may be considered for ongoing care and treatment of chronic hepatitis if diagnosed.
