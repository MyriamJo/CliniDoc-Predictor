HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
A 16-year-old male presents to the emergency room after experiencing a syncopal episode while playing sports. He reports feeling lightheaded and dizzy before losing consciousness for a few seconds. He denies any prodromal symptoms such as palpitations or chest pain. This is the first episode of syncope he has experienced. Upon regaining consciousness, he complains of mild headache and fatigue.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
No significant past medical history reported. No history of cardiac conditions or previous syncopal episodes.

PAST SURGICAL HISTORY:
Nonsignificant.

SOCIAL HISTORY:
Active participation in sports activities. Denies alcohol, drugs, or tobacco use.

FAMILY HISTORY:
Negative for cardiac disorders or sudden cardiac death.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Vital Signs: Within normal limits.
General: Alert and oriented, no acute distress.
Cardiovascular: Regular heart rate and rhythm, no murmurs or gallops appreciated.
Neurological: Cranial nerves intact, no focal deficits.

ASSESSMENT:

Syncope: Suspected vasovagal syncope based on the history of sudden loss of consciousness without prodromal symptoms or cardiac abnormalities on exam.
PLAN:

Cardiac Evaluation: Recommend further cardiac evaluation, including an electrocardiogram (ECG) and Holter monitor, to rule out any underlying arrhythmias or structural heart abnormalities.
Lifestyle Modification: Advise the patient to stay hydrated, avoid prolonged standing, and consider modifying sports activities until further evaluation is completed.
Follow-up: Schedule a follow-up appointment in two weeks to review test results and assess the need for additional interventions or referrals.
