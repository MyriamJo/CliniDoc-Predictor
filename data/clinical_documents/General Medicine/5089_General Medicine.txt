HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
An 82-year-old male presents with progressive memory impairment and cognitive decline over the past year. He reports forgetting recent events, misplacing belongings, and experiencing difficulty with daily tasks such as managing finances or cooking. Family members note personality changes, social withdrawal, and decreased interest in previously enjoyed activities. The patient denies any history of head trauma or psychiatric disorders.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
History of hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and type 2 diabetes mellitus. No history of stroke or neurodegenerative disorders.

PAST SURGICAL HISTORY:
Nonsignificant.

FAMILY HISTORY:
Positive for Alzheimer's disease in a first-degree relative.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Vital Signs: Within normal limits.
Neurological: Mild cognitive impairment evidenced by impaired short-term memory, decreased attention span, and word-finding difficulties. Cranial nerves intact, no focal neurological deficits.

ASSESSMENT:

Mild Cognitive Impairment: Suspected early-stage Alzheimer's disease based on the patient's progressive memory loss and cognitive decline, along with positive family history.
PLAN:

Neuropsychological Evaluation: Refer the patient for comprehensive neuropsychological testing to assess cognitive function and aid in diagnosis.
Medication Management: Initiate pharmacotherapy with cholinesterase inhibitors or memantine to slow cognitive decline and improve symptoms.
Caregiver Support: Offer resources and support services for family caregivers to assist with the patient's care needs and provide education on coping strategies.
Advanced Directives: Discuss advance care planning and document the patient's preferences for future medical care.
Follow-up: Schedule regular follow-up appointments to monitor disease progression, adjust treatment as needed, and provide ongoing support for the patient and family.
