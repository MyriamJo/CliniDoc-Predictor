Procedure:
Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)

Reason for Procedure:

The patient exhibited symptoms of obstructive jaundice and elevated liver enzymes, suggesting a possible biliary obstruction.

Operative Details:

ERCP was performed with the patient under general anesthesia. The endoscope was advanced to the duodenum, and the ampulla of Vater was cannulated. Cholangiography revealed a stricture in the common bile duct with proximal dilatation. A biliary sphincterotomy was performed, followed by the placement of a plastic stent to ensure biliary drainage. Bile duct brushings were taken for cytological analysis.

Impression:

ERCP findings consistent with a common bile duct stricture, successfully managed with biliary sphincterotomy and stent placement. Further evaluation of the stricture is required to determine its etiology.

Post-procedure Care:

Post-ERCP, the patient was monitored for signs of pancreatitis, infection, or perforation. Antibiotics were administered prophylactically. The patient was advised to maintain a clear liquid diet for 24 hours before gradually resuming a regular diet. Instructions were given to monitor for symptoms of post-ERCP pancreatitis, such as severe abdominal pain, nausea, or vomiting.

Follow-up:

A follow-up appointment was arranged to evaluate the patient's clinical response to the procedure and to discuss the cytology results of the bile duct brushings. If necessary, additional imaging or interventions would be planned based on the findings. Regular monitoring of liver function tests was advised to assess ongoing biliary drainage.
