Chief Complaint:
The patient presents with persistent cough and shortness of breath.

History of Present Illness:
A 45-year-old male reports experiencing a chronic cough and shortness of breath for the past three months. The cough is dry, non-productive, and more pronounced at night. He also notes occasional wheezing and a feeling of tightness in his chest. The symptoms are exacerbated by physical exertion and exposure to cold air. He denies any history of fever, chills, or significant weight loss. The patient has tried over-the-counter cough suppressants with minimal relief.

Past Medical History:
No significant past medical history reported. No history of asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).

Family History:
Negative for respiratory disorders.

Review of Systems:
Negative for fever, night sweats, or other constitutional symptoms. No gastrointestinal or genitourinary symptoms reported.

Physical Examination:

Vital Signs: Within normal limits.
Respiratory: Lungs clear to auscultation bilaterally with no wheezes, rales, or rhonchi. No use of accessory muscles for breathing noted.
Cardiovascular: Normal S1 and S2 with no murmurs, rubs, or gallops.
Assessment:

Chronic Cough and Dyspnea: Suspected diagnosis based on the patient's history of persistent cough and shortness of breath without evidence of infection or significant past medical history.
Differential Diagnosis: Consideration given to conditions such as asthma, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), and postnasal drip syndrome.

Plan:

Pulmonary Function Tests (PFTs):
Order PFTs, including spirometry, to assess for obstructive or restrictive lung disease.

Trial of Inhaled Bronchodilators:
Consider initiating a trial of inhaled bronchodilators such as albuterol to evaluate for potential improvement in symptoms, indicative of asthma.

GERD Evaluation:
Recommend an empirical trial of a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) to assess for potential gastroesophageal reflux contributing to the chronic cough.

Chest X-ray:
Order a chest X-ray to rule out any underlying pulmonary pathology.

Follow-up:
Schedule a follow-up appointment in four weeks to review test results and assess response to treatment. Adjust management as needed based on the findings and patient's symptom progression.

Patient Education:
Educate the patient on avoiding known triggers such as cold air and physical exertion until a definitive diagnosis is made. Encourage smoking cessation if applicable and provide resources for support.
