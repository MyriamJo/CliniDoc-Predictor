Chief Complaint:

"I've been feeling lightheaded and my heart races sometimes."

History of Present Illness:

A 16-year-old adolescent presents with complaints of lightheadedness and occasional palpitations. He reports experiencing episodes of feeling dizzy and lightheaded, particularly when transitioning from sitting to standing or during physical exertion. Additionally, he describes occasional episodes of palpitations, where he feels his heart racing or pounding in his chest. These symptoms have been occurring intermittently over the past few weeks. He denies any chest pain, shortness of breath, or syncope.

Past Medical History:

The patient has no significant past medical history of cardiovascular diseases or chronic illnesses.

Family History:

There is a family history of hypertension on the maternal side, but no history of congenital heart diseases or arrhythmias.

Social History:

The patient is an active teenager who participates in sports and recreational activities. He denies tobacco, alcohol, or illicit drug use. He lives with his parents and attends school regularly.

Medications:

The patient is not currently taking any medications.

Review of Systems:

Negative for chest pain, shortness of breath, syncope, leg swelling, or significant weight changes.

Physical Examination:

Vital Signs: Blood pressure within normal range for age, heart rate regular, respiratory rate normal.
General: Well-developed adolescent in no acute distress.
Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs, rubs, or gallops appreciated. Peripheral pulses are intact.
Neurological: Cranial nerves intact, no focal deficits.
Musculoskeletal: No signs of joint swelling or deformities.
Skin: No rashes or lesions observed.

Assessment:

1. Lightheadedness: Episodes of dizziness and lightheadedness, particularly with positional changes, suggestive of orthostatic hypotension or vasovagal syncope.
2. Palpitations: Occasional episodes of palpitations warrant further evaluation to rule out cardiac arrhythmias or other underlying cardiac issues.

Plan:

1. Further Evaluation: The patient will undergo diagnostic tests, including an electrocardiogram (ECG) and ambulatory Holter monitoring to assess cardiac rhythm and detect any arrhythmias.
2. Lifestyle Modifications: Encourage the patient to maintain adequate hydration and salt intake, and to avoid prolonged periods of standing or sudden changes in position.
3. Symptom Management: Provide reassurance and educate the patient on techniques to manage symptoms, such as deep breathing exercises and avoiding triggers.
4. Follow-up: Schedule a follow-up appointment to review the results of diagnostic tests and discuss further management options based on findings.

Follow-up:

The patient will return for follow-up to discuss the results of diagnostic tests and develop a comprehensive management plan tailored to address his symptoms and optimize his cardiovascular health. Continued monitoring and support will be provided to ensure his well-being and symptom control.
