Chief Complaint:

"I've been having abdominal pain and bloating after eating."

History of Present Illness:

A 28-year-old woman presents with complaints of abdominal pain and bloating after eating meals. She describes the pain as a dull, crampy sensation in the lower abdomen, accompanied by excessive gas and bloating. The symptoms have been present for the past few months and tend to worsen after consuming certain foods, particularly those high in fiber or fat. She denies any changes in bowel habits, diarrhea, or constipation.

Past Medical History:

The patient has a history of irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) and lactose intolerance, which she manages with dietary modifications.

Family History:

There is no significant family history of gastrointestinal diseases or malignancies.

Social History:

The patient maintains a healthy diet and exercises regularly. She does not smoke or consume alcohol excessively.

Medications:

The patient takes over-the-counter lactase supplements as needed for her lactose intolerance.

Review of Systems:

Negative for fever, weight loss, blood in stool, or vomiting. No history of recent travel or antibiotic use.

Physical Examination:

Vital Signs: Blood pressure within normal range, heart rate regular, respiratory rate normal.
General: Well-appearing woman in no acute distress.
Abdomen: Soft, non-tender, non-distended. No palpable masses or organomegaly. Normal bowel sounds.

Assessment:

Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS): Symptoms of abdominal pain, bloating, and changes in bowel habits are consistent with IBS, exacerbated by certain dietary triggers.
Lactose Intolerance: History of lactose intolerance suggests possible exacerbation of symptoms with dairy consumption.
Plan:

Dietary Modification: Recommend keeping a food diary to identify specific triggers and avoid foods that worsen symptoms, such as high-fiber or high-fat foods.
Symptomatic Relief: Encourage the use of over-the-counter medications such as simethicone for gas and bloating relief, as well as probiotics to promote gut health.
Lactase Supplements: Continue using lactase supplements as needed to manage symptoms related to lactose intolerance.
Follow-up: Schedule a follow-up appointment to assess response to dietary modifications and adjust management plan as needed.
Follow-up:

The patient will return for follow-up to review her food diary and discuss further management strategies based on symptom control and dietary adjustments.
