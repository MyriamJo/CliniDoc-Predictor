Subjective:

The patient, a 38-year-old woman, presents with complaints of persistent abdominal pain and discomfort. She describes the pain as dull and achy, primarily located in the lower abdomen, which has been gradually worsening over the past week. She also reports experiencing bloating, occasional nausea, and changes in bowel habits, including alternating diarrhea and constipation. The patient denies any recent fever, vomiting, or rectal bleeding.

Past Medical History:

The patient has a history of irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) with constipation, diagnosed five years ago.

Family History:

Negative for significant gastrointestinal disorders.

Objective:

General: The patient appears uncomfortable but not in acute distress.

Abdomen: Mild distension noted, with tenderness to palpation in the lower quadrants. No guarding or rebound tenderness. Bowel sounds present in all quadrants.

**Rectal Examination: No masses, hemorrhoids, or evidence of rectal bleeding. Stool guaiac test negative.

Pelvic Examination: Within normal limits, no palpable masses or tenderness.

Assessment:

The patient is assessed with an exacerbation of irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), presenting with abdominal pain, bloating, and altered bowel habits.

Plan:

Management plan includes:
- Symptomatic relief with antispasmodic medication for abdominal pain.
- Dietary modifications, emphasizing a low-FODMAP diet and increased fiber intake.
- Stress reduction techniques, such as mindfulness and relaxation exercises.
- Follow-up appointment in two weeks to assess response to treatment and adjust management as needed. Referral to gastroenterology for further evaluation may be considered if symptoms persist or worsen despite initial management.
