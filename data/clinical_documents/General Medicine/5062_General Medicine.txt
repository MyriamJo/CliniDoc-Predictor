Subjective:

The patient, a 6-year-old girl, is brought in by her parents due to the sudden onset of a rash. According to her parents, the rash appeared this morning and has progressively worsened throughout the day. They report that the rash initially started on her trunk and has since spread to her arms and legs. The parents deny any recent illnesses, insect bites, or exposure to new skincare products or detergents.

Past Medical History:

The patient has no significant medical history of chronic conditions or allergies.

Family History:

Negative for significant dermatological conditions or allergies.

Objective:

General: The patient appears otherwise well, with no signs of distress.

Skin Examination: The rash appears as erythematous patches with raised borders, scattered throughout the trunk, arms, and legs. The lesions vary in size and shape, with some exhibiting central clearing. There is no associated itching, pain, or tenderness. No signs of blistering or ulceration are noted.

Assessment:

The patient is diagnosed with an acute onset rash, possibly of viral or allergic etiology.

Plan:

Management plan includes:
- Symptomatic relief with topical antihistamine cream to alleviate any associated itching or discomfort.
- Oral antihistamines may be prescribed for additional symptom relief if itching becomes bothersome.
- Advising the parents to monitor the rash for any changes in appearance or progression. They are instructed to seek medical attention promptly if there are any signs of worsening or systemic symptoms.
- Follow-up appointment scheduled in a few days for reassessment of the rash and to ensure resolution. Referral to dermatology may be considered if the rash persists or if there are any concerning features.
