Title of Procedure:
Lumbar Puncture for Evaluation of Guillain-Barré Syndrome

Indication for Procedure:

A 55-year-old male presented with rapidly progressive weakness, starting in the lower extremities and ascending symmetrically. Clinical examination revealed areflexia, sensory deficits, and bilateral facial weakness. Suspected diagnosis of Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) prompted the recommendation for a lumbar puncture to assess cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) protein levels and aid in the diagnosis and management of the condition.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Suspected Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS).

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS).

Procedure:

The patient was positioned in the lateral decubitus position, and the lower back was prepped and draped in a sterile manner. Local anesthesia was administered, and a spinal needle was inserted into the subarachnoid space between the lumbar vertebrae. CSF was collected and sent for analysis, focusing on protein levels, cell count, and examination for oligoclonal bands.

Impression:

Lumbar puncture revealed elevated protein levels with normal cell count in the cerebrospinal fluid, consistent with an albuminocytologic dissociation characteristic of Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS). The absence of oligoclonal bands supported the diagnosis and ruled out other inflammatory or infectious etiologies.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was admitted to the neurology service for further evaluation and management of Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS). Treatment options, including intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) or plasma exchange therapy, were discussed based on disease severity and progression. Close monitoring for respiratory and autonomic dysfunction was initiated to prevent complications.
