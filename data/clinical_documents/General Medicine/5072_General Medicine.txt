Chief Complaint:

"I'm still having trouble sleeping even with the CPAP and oxygen."

History of Present Illness:

A 52-year-old woman with a history of polyarteritis nodosa, mononeuritis multiplex, and severe sleep apnea presents for follow-up regarding ongoing sleep disturbances despite treatment. She reports persistent difficulty initiating and maintaining sleep despite being on continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) and oxygen therapy. The patient describes restless sleep, frequent awakenings, and a feeling of unrefreshing sleep quality. She also reports exacerbation of leg pain from mononeuritis multiplex and continued use of Lasix for lower extremity edema management. Recent weight gain of 10 pounds and increased fatigue further compound her symptoms.

Medications:

The patient continues on prednisone, Ativan, imipramine, MS Contin, immediate-release morphine, and Lasix. Prednisone dosage has been adjusted, and opiates are used for pain relief.

Findings:

Vital signs reveal elevated blood pressure, weight gain, and resting hypoxemia. Lower extremity examination demonstrates pretibial edema with erythema.

Laboratory and Diagnostic Findings:

Arterial blood gas analysis confirms a compensated respiratory acidosis. CPAP compliance monitoring indicates suboptimal adherence, and the overnight sleep study reveals persistent severe central sleep apnea.

Assessment:

1. Persistent obstructive sleep apnea despite CPAP therapy, likely aggravated by central sleep apnea and opiate use.
2. Chronic pain from mononeuritis multiplex necessitating high-dose analgesics.
3. Hypoxemia attributed to obesity hypoventilation syndrome, hypoventilation, and possible pulmonary pathology.
4. Aggravated lower extremity edema due to polyarteritis nodosa and Lasix therapy.

Plan:

1. Adjustment of pain management regimen with a focus on minimizing evening opiate doses.
2. Consideration of adjunctive therapies for sleep apnea, including the addition of bilevel positive airway pressure (BiPAP) or adaptive servo-ventilation (ASV).
3. Optimization of Lasix therapy to enhance diuresis and alleviate lower extremity edema.
4. Further evaluation with pulmonary function tests and imaging studies to assess for underlying pulmonary pathology contributing to hypoxemia.
5. Implementation of lifestyle modifications, including weight management strategies and sleep hygiene practices.

Follow-up:

The patient will undergo a trial of adjunctive sleep apnea therapy and close monitoring of symptoms, medication adherence, and treatment response. Regular follow-up appointments will be scheduled to assess progress and adjust management strategies as needed. Emphasis will be placed on comprehensive care coordination to optimize overall health outcomes and improve quality of life.
