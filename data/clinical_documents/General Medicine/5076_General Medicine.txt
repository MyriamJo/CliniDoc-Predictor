Chief Complaint:

"I've been experiencing difficulty urinating and lower abdominal pain."

History of Present Illness:

A 55-year-old man presents with complaints of difficulty urinating and lower abdominal pain. He reports a sensation of incomplete emptying of the bladder and a weak urinary stream. Additionally, he describes intermittent episodes of urinary urgency and nocturia, waking up multiple times at night to urinate. He denies any gross hematuria, urinary retention, or urinary incontinence.

Past Medical History:

The patient has a history of benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) and hypertension, for which he takes medications regularly. He has no history of urinary tract infections or kidney stones.

Family History:

There is a family history of prostate cancer on his father's side, but no other significant urological diseases.

Social History:

The patient is a non-smoker and does not consume alcohol excessively. He works as an accountant and leads a sedentary lifestyle, but tries to stay active by walking daily.

Medications:

The patient takes the following medications for his BPH and hypertension: tamsulosin and lisinopril.

Review of Systems:

Negative for fever, chills, weight loss, or back pain. No history of recent travel or exposure to infectious diseases.

Physical Examination:

Vital Signs: Blood pressure within normal range, heart rate regular, respiratory rate normal.
General: Well-appearing man in no acute distress.
Abdomen: Soft, non-tender, non-distended. No palpable masses or organomegaly.
Genitourinary: Enlarged prostate appreciated on digital rectal examination. No scrotal masses or tenderness noted.

Assessment:

Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia: Symptoms suggestive of BPH include difficulty urinating, weak urinary stream, urinary urgency, and nocturia.
Rule Out Prostate Cancer: Family history of prostate cancer warrants consideration for further evaluation to rule out malignancy.
Hypertension: Control of blood pressure is important for overall cardiovascular health.
Plan:

Further Evaluation: The patient will undergo diagnostic tests, including prostate-specific antigen (PSA) blood test and prostate ultrasound, to assess prostate health and rule out prostate cancer.
Medication Adjustment: Review and potentially adjust current medications for BPH and hypertension to optimize symptom control and blood pressure management.
Lifestyle Modifications: Encourage the patient to maintain hydration, avoid excessive caffeine intake, and engage in regular physical activity to promote urinary and overall health.
Follow-up:

The patient will return for follow-up to review the results of diagnostic tests and discuss further management options based on findings. Close monitoring and ongoing support will be provided to address his urological concerns and promote overall well-being.
