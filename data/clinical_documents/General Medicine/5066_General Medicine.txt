Chief Complaint:

"I've been experiencing intense abdominal pain and unusual swelling in my legs."

History of Present Illness:

A 35-year-old woman presents with complaints of severe abdominal pain localized in the lower abdomen, along with progressive swelling in both legs. The pain started abruptly two days ago and has been constant since then, exacerbating with movement. She describes the pain as sharp and stabbing, rated 8 out of 10 on the pain scale, and is not relieved by over-the-counter pain medications. Additionally, she notices swelling in her legs, which has been worsening over the past week, making it difficult for her to walk comfortably. She denies any recent trauma, fever, or changes in bowel habits.

Past Medical History:

The patient has a history of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) diagnosed five years ago, currently managed with hydroxychloroquine.

Family History:

Negative for significant medical conditions.

Social History:

The patient works as a nurse in a local hospital. She does not smoke or consume alcohol. She is sexually active with her long-term partner and uses barrier contraception consistently.

Medications:

Hydroxychloroquine for systemic lupus erythematosus.

Allergies:

No known drug allergies.

Objective:

General: The patient appears uncomfortable due to abdominal pain but is alert and oriented.

Abdominal Examination: On palpation, there is tenderness and guarding in the lower abdomen, particularly in the left iliac fossa. No rebound tenderness or palpable masses are appreciated. Bowel sounds are normal.

Peripheral Edema: Bilateral pitting edema present in both legs, extending from the ankles to the knees. The skin overlying the edematous areas appears tense and shiny.

Assessment:

The patient is suspected to have a flare-up of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) with possible lupus nephritis, given the abdominal pain and lower extremity edema in the context of her medical history.

Plan:

Management plan includes:
- Immediate referral to rheumatology for further evaluation and management of SLE flare-up.
- Laboratory investigations, including complete blood count (CBC), renal function tests, urine analysis, and urine protein-to-creatinine ratio to assess for lupus nephritis.
- Symptomatic relief with nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for abdominal pain and compression stockings for lower extremity edema.
- Education on the importance of regular follow-up and adherence to prescribed medications for the management of SLE.
- Follow-up appointment scheduled in one week to monitor response to treatment and adjust management as needed. Referral to nephrology may be considered if lupus nephritis is confirmed.
