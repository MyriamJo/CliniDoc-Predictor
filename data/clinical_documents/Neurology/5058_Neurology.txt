Title of Procedure: Electromyography-Guided Sacroiliac Joint Injection for Chronic Low Back Pain

Indication for Procedure:

A 45-year-old female presented with chronic low back pain localized to the sacroiliac joint region. Clinical examination revealed tenderness over the sacroiliac joint and positive provocative maneuvers. Electromyography (EMG) was recommended to confirm joint involvement and guide targeted intervention.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Sacroiliac joint dysfunction.

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Sacroiliac joint dysfunction.

Procedure:

The patient was positioned comfortably in a prone position. Following skin preparation, needle electrodes were inserted adjacent to the sacroiliac joint to assess the electrical activity and dysfunction of the surrounding muscles.

Under real-time EMG guidance, a mixture of local anesthetic and corticosteroid was injected into the sacroiliac joint to provide pain relief and reduce inflammation. The injection was performed using fluoroscopic guidance to ensure accurate needle placement and optimal medication delivery.

Impression:

EMG findings support the diagnosis of sacroiliac joint dysfunction. Electromyography-guided sacroiliac joint injection performed for targeted pain relief.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was advised on post-procedural care, including activity modification and potential temporary exacerbation of symptoms. Physical therapy and home exercises were recommended to improve joint stability and function. Follow-up appointments were scheduled to monitor pain relief and assess the need for further interventions.
