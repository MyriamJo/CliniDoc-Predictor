Chief Complaint:

"I've been experiencing chest discomfort and shortness of breath lately."

History of Present Illness:

A 42-year-old woman presents with complaints of chest discomfort and shortness of breath that have been bothering her lately. She describes the chest discomfort as a tightness or pressure in the center of her chest that comes and goes. She also reports feeling short of breath, especially with exertion or when climbing stairs. These symptoms have been present for the past month and have been gradually worsening. She denies any palpitations, dizziness, or syncope.

Past Medical History:

The patient has a history of hypertension and dyslipidemia, for which she takes medications regularly. She has no history of diabetes, heart disease, or significant surgeries.

Family History:

There is a family history of coronary artery disease on her father's side, with her father having experienced a heart attack in his early 50s.

Social History:

The patient is a non-smoker and does not consume alcohol excessively. She works as an office manager and leads a relatively sedentary lifestyle, but tries to stay active by walking regularly.

Medications:

The patient takes the following medications for her hypertension and dyslipidemia: lisinopril, atorvastatin, and low-dose aspirin.

Review of Systems:

Negative for fever, cough, leg swelling, or recent weight changes. No history of recent illness or travel.

Physical Examination:

Vital Signs: Blood pressure within normal range, heart rate regular, respiratory rate normal.
General: Well-appearing woman in no acute distress.
Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs, rubs, or gallops appreciated. Peripheral pulses are intact.
Respiratory: Clear lung fields bilaterally, no wheezes or crackles.
Abdomen: Soft, non-tender, non-distended.
Extremities: No cyanosis, clubbing, or edema noted.
Skin: No rashes or lesions observed.

Assessment:

1. Chest Discomfort: Symptoms of chest tightness or pressure warrant further evaluation to rule out cardiac causes such as angina or myocardial infarction, especially given the patient's risk factors and family history.
2. Shortness of Breath: Exertional dyspnea could be indicative of underlying cardiac or pulmonary issues, or deconditioning.

Plan:

1. Further Evaluation: The patient will undergo diagnostic tests, including an electrocardiogram (ECG), stress test, and possibly cardiac imaging such as echocardiography, to assess cardiac function and rule out coronary artery disease.
2. Lifestyle Modifications: Encourage the patient to engage in regular physical activity and adopt heart-healthy habits, such as maintaining a balanced diet, managing stress, and quitting smoking if applicable.
3. Medication Review: Review the patient's current medications to ensure optimization of blood pressure and lipid control.
4. Patient Education: Provide education on recognizing cardiac symptoms and when to seek immediate medical attention, as well as strategies for reducing cardiovascular risk factors.

Follow-up:

The patient will return for follow-up to review the results of diagnostic tests and discuss further management options based on findings. Close monitoring and ongoing support will be provided to address her cardiovascular health concerns and promote overall well-being.
