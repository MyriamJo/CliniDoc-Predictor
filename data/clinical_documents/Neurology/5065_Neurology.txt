Title of Procedure:
Electromyography-Guided Botulinum Toxin Injection for Chronic Hemifacial Spasm

Indication for Procedure:

A 50-year-old female presented with involuntary muscle contractions affecting the left side of her face, consistent with hemifacial spasm. Clinical examination revealed repetitive, synchronous contractions of the facial muscles, including the orbicularis oculi and zygomaticus major. Electromyography (EMG) was recommended to assess muscle activity and guide targeted treatment.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Left-sided hemifacial spasm.

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Left-sided hemifacial spasm.

Procedure:

The patient was positioned comfortably in a seated position. Following skin preparation, needle electrodes were inserted into the affected facial muscles to assess their electrical activity and degree of hyperactivity.

Under real-time EMG guidance, botulinum toxin was injected into the overactive muscles, targeting specific motor endplates to induce localized chemical denervation. Multiple injection sites were selected based on EMG findings to ensure comprehensive coverage of the affected muscle groups.

Impression:

EMG findings support the diagnosis of left-sided hemifacial spasm. Electromyography-guided botulinum toxin injection performed for targeted relaxation of overactive facial muscles.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was monitored post-injection for immediate effects and potential adverse reactions. Instructions were given regarding facial exercises to maintain muscle tone and prevent facial weakness. Follow-up appointments were scheduled to assess treatment response, adjust dosage if necessary, and plan for subsequent injections.
