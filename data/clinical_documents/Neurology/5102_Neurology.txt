Title of Procedure:
Lumbar Puncture for Evaluation of Suspected Neurosyphilis

Indication for Procedure:

A 50-year-old male presented with neurological symptoms including cognitive decline, gait disturbances, and visual disturbances. Clinical evaluation raised suspicion for neurosyphilis, a late-stage manifestation of syphilis affecting the central nervous system. Lumbar puncture was recommended to assess cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) for the presence of treponemal antibodies and aid in the diagnosis of neurosyphilis.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Suspected neurosyphilis.

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Neurosyphilis.

Procedure:

The patient was positioned in the lateral decubitus position, and the lumbar region was prepped and draped in a sterile manner. After local anesthesia, a spinal needle was inserted into the subarachnoid space, and CSF was collected and sent for analysis. CSF samples were evaluated for the presence of treponemal antibodies using serological tests such as the Venereal Disease Research Laboratory (VDRL) test and fluorescent treponemal antibody absorption (FTA-ABS) test.

Impression:

Lumbar puncture revealed the presence of treponemal antibodies in the cerebrospinal fluid, consistent with a diagnosis of neurosyphilis. The findings supported the clinical suspicion of neurosyphilis, corroborating the patient's presentation of neurological symptoms in the setting of untreated syphilis infection.
