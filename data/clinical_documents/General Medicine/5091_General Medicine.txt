HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
A 45-year-old female presents with acute-onset left shoulder pain after lifting heavy boxes while moving furniture. She describes the pain as sharp and localized to the anterior aspect of the shoulder, exacerbated by movement and relieved by rest. She denies any associated numbness, tingling, or weakness in the affected arm. No history of trauma or recent shoulder injuries reported.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
History of osteoarthritis in the knees, managed conservatively with occasional nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).

PAST SURGICAL HISTORY:
Nonsignificant.

FAMILY HISTORY:
Negative for any significant musculoskeletal disorders.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Vital Signs: Within normal limits.
Musculoskeletal: Tender to palpation over the left anterior shoulder, limited range of motion with abduction and external rotation, no instability appreciated.
Neurological: Intact sensation and motor function in the left upper extremity, normal reflexes.

ASSESSMENT:

Acute Shoulder Strain: Suspected acute musculoskeletal injury secondary to lifting heavy objects, leading to strain or sprain of the shoulder muscles or ligaments.
PLAN:

Symptomatic Treatment: Recommend rest, ice application, and over-the-counter NSAIDs for pain relief and inflammation control.
Activity Modification: Advise avoiding heavy lifting or strenuous activities involving the affected shoulder until symptoms resolve.
Physical Therapy: Consider referral to physical therapy for range of motion exercises and strengthening once acute pain subsides.
Follow-up: Schedule a follow-up appointment in two weeks to assess response to conservative management and discuss further treatment options if necessary.
