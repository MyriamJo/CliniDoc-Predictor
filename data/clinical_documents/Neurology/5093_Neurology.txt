Title of Procedure: 
Lumbar Puncture for Evaluation of Central Nervous System Infection

Indication for Procedure:

A 30-year-old female presented with fever, headache, photophobia, and neck stiffness. Clinical examination revealed nuchal rigidity and Kernig's sign. Suspected diagnosis of meningitis prompted the recommendation for a lumbar puncture to obtain cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) for analysis and aid in the diagnosis and management of the infection.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Suspected meningitis or encephalitis.

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Meningitis.

Procedure:

The patient was positioned in a seated or lateral decubitus position, and the lower back was prepped and draped in a sterile manner. Local anesthesia was administered, and a spinal needle was inserted into the subarachnoid space between the lumbar vertebrae. CSF was collected and sent for analysis, including cell count, protein levels, glucose levels, and culture.

Impression:

Lumbar puncture revealed elevated white blood cell count, elevated protein levels, and decreased glucose levels in the cerebrospinal fluid, consistent with a diagnosis of bacterial meningitis. CSF culture confirmed the presence of Streptococcus pneumoniae, guiding appropriate antimicrobial therapy.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was initiated on empiric antibiotic therapy pending CSF culture results. Symptomatic management, including analgesics for headache and antipyretics for fever, was provided. Close monitoring for signs of neurological deterioration and response to treatment was instituted, with plans for repeat lumbar puncture if necessary to assess treatment response.
