Medical Record:

Procedure: Gallbladder Ultrasound (Sonography)

Reason for Procedure:

Recurrent bouts of intense upper right abdominal pain, accompanied by fever and yellowing of the skin, indicating a potential acute cholecystitis or obstruction of the bile ducts.

Ultrasound Findings:

The gallbladder ultrasound was promptly conducted to investigate the patient's distressing symptoms. The examination revealed the following observations:

1. Gallstones: Numerous bright spots scattered throughout the gallbladder, suggestive of multiple gallstones of varying sizes. Some of these stones measured up to 2 centimeters in diameter.

2. Gallbladder Wall Thickening: The gallbladder walls appeared noticeably swollen, measuring approximately 8 millimeters. This suggests an acute inflammatory response, consistent with acute cholecystitis.

3. Dilated Common Bile Duct (CBD): The bile duct, responsible for transporting bile from the liver to the small intestine, appeared wider than normal, measuring up to 1.5 centimeters in diameter. This dilation could indicate a potential blockage, raising concerns of choledocholithiasis or obstructive pathology.

Impression:

The gallbladder ultrasound findings strongly indicate acute cholecystitis with associated gallstones and a possible obstruction in the common bile duct. The presence of gallstones, swollen gallbladder walls, and dilated bile ducts necessitates urgent attention and intervention.

Recommendations:

1. Immediate Medical Assessment: Urgent consultation with a gastroenterologist or surgeon is imperative for a thorough evaluation and management plan for acute cholecystitis and potential bile duct obstruction. Prompt initiation of antibiotics, pain relief, and intravenous fluids may be essential for stabilizing the patient and addressing the underlying infection.

2. Consideration for Advanced Imaging: Further assessment with procedures such as Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) or Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) may be warranted to evaluate the bile ducts for any obstructions or stones.

3. Surgical Evaluation: Discussion regarding the necessity of gallbladder removal (cholecystectomy) and potential interventions for addressing bile duct obstruction or stones should be promptly addressed to mitigate the risk of complications and alleviate the patient's distress.

Follow-up:

The patient requires immediate hospitalization for comprehensive evaluation and management of acute cholecystitis and potential bile duct obstruction. Close monitoring and timely intervention are essential to prevent complications and facilitate the patient's recovery.
