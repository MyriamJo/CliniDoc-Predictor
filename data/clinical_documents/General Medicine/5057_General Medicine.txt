Reason for Consultation:

A 45-year-old man presents for evaluation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) exacerbation and dyspnea.

History of Present Illness:

The patient reports a history of COPD with frequent exacerbations, characterized by worsening dyspnea, cough, and sputum production. He notes increased shortness of breath over the past week, limiting his ability to perform daily activities. He also reports a productive cough with yellowish-green sputum.

The patient denies any chest pain, palpitations, or lower extremity swelling. He also denies any recent fevers, chills, or night sweats.

Coronary Risk Factors:

The patient has a history of smoking for 30 years but quit smoking 5 years ago. He has a family history of coronary artery disease.

Family History:

Positive for coronary artery disease.

Medications:

1. Albuterol inhaler for COPD exacerbations.
2. Tiotropium inhaler for maintenance therapy.
3. Fluticasone/salmeterol inhaler for maintenance therapy.
4. Lisinopril for hypertension.
5. Atorvastatin for hyperlipidemia.
6. Aspirin for cardiovascular prophylaxis.

Allergies:

None reported.

Personal History:

The patient is a former smoker, having quit 5 years ago. He denies alcohol consumption and recreational drug use.

Past Medical History:

Significant for COPD, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia.

Review of Systems:

Constitutional: Fatigue and dyspnea.
Respiratory: Increased dyspnea, productive cough.
Cardiovascular: No chest pain or palpitations.
Gastrointestinal: No gastrointestinal symptoms.
Neurological: No focal weakness or sensory changes.
Psychological: No anxiety or depression.

Physical Examination:

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 140/90 mmHg, pulse 90 bpm, respiratory rate 24/min, temperature 98.6°F.
General: Appears fatigued, using accessory respiratory muscles.
Respiratory: Increased work of breathing, diminished breath sounds with scattered wheezes bilaterally.
Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs.
Abdomen: Soft, nontender, nondistended.
Extremities: No clubbing or cyanosis, peripheral pulses intact.

Assessment and Plan:

The patient's presentation is consistent with a COPD exacerbation, characterized by worsening dyspnea, cough, and sputum production.

Immediate management will include bronchodilator therapy with albuterol and systemic corticosteroids to alleviate bronchospasm and reduce airway inflammation. Oxygen therapy will be initiated to maintain adequate oxygenation.

Assessment and optimization of cardiovascular risk factors, including hypertension and hyperlipidemia, will be continued. Assessment for underlying cardiac disease will be considered if symptoms persist despite adequate COPD management.

The patient will be monitored closely for improvement in symptoms and signs of respiratory distress. Close follow-up appointments will be scheduled to ensure resolution of the exacerbation and optimize long-term COPD management.

Smoking cessation counseling and support will be provided to promote continued abstinence from tobacco. Referral to pulmonary rehabilitation and smoking cessation programs may be considered to further improve respiratory function and overall health.
