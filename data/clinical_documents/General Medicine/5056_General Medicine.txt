Reason for Consultation:

A 62-year-old woman is referred for evaluation of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) and recent onset of joint pain and swelling.

History of Present Illness:

The patient presents with complaints of progressively worsening joint pain and swelling, primarily affecting her hands and wrists. She describes morning stiffness lasting for over an hour, which gradually improves throughout the day with activity. She reports difficulty with fine motor tasks such as buttoning clothes and holding utensils due to pain and stiffness in her hands.

Additionally, the patient notes fatigue and malaise, which have been interfering with her daily activities. She reports feeling generally unwell and has experienced a loss of appetite in recent weeks.

Coronary Risk Factors:

The patient has a history of hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and a family history of coronary artery disease.

Family History:

Positive for coronary artery disease.

Medications:

1. Methotrexate for rheumatoid arthritis.
2. Prednisone for rheumatoid arthritis flare-ups.
3. Lisinopril for hypertension.
4. Atorvastatin for hyperlipidemia.
5. Folic acid supplementation.

Allergies:

None reported.

Personal History:

The patient is a former smoker, having quit 10 years ago. She consumes alcohol occasionally and denies recreational drug use.

Past Medical History:

Significant for osteoporosis, for which she takes calcium and vitamin D supplements. She has a history of benign breast masses and has undergone mammography surveillance.

Review of Systems:

Constitutional: Fatigue and malaise.
Musculoskeletal: Joint pain and swelling, morning stiffness.
Gastrointestinal: Loss of appetite.
Cardiovascular: As above.
Respiratory: No respiratory symptoms.
Neurological: No focal weakness or sensory changes.
Psychological: No anxiety or depression.

Physical Examination:

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 140/80 mmHg, pulse 72 bpm, respiratory rate 16/min, temperature 98.6°F.
General: Alert and oriented, appearing fatigued.
Musculoskeletal: Symmetrical joint swelling and tenderness in the hands and wrists, limited range of motion due to pain and stiffness.
Skin: No rash or skin lesions.
Neurological: Cranial nerves intact, normal motor and sensory function.
Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs.
Respiratory: Clear breath sounds bilaterally.
Abdomen: Soft, nontender, nondistended.
Extremities: No clubbing or cyanosis, peripheral pulses intact.

Assessment and Plan:

The patient's presentation is consistent with a flare-up of rheumatoid arthritis, characterized by joint pain, swelling, morning stiffness, and fatigue.

Management will include optimization of rheumatoid arthritis therapy with methotrexate and short-term prednisone to control the current flare-up. Close monitoring of disease activity and side effects of medications will be performed.

Assessment and management of cardiovascular risk factors, including hypertension and hyperlipidemia, will be continued. Lifestyle modifications, including smoking cessation and dietary changes, will be emphasized.

Routine monitoring for osteoporosis will be maintained, with consideration for additional bone density testing as indicated.

Follow-up appointments will be scheduled to monitor the patient's response to treatment and adjust management strategies as needed. Referral to a rheumatologist may be considered for further evaluation and management of rheumatoid arthritis.
