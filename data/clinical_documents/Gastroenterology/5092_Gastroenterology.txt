Procedure:
Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD)

Reason for Procedure:

The patient presented with recurrent epigastric pain and suspected gastrointestinal bleeding.

Operative Details:

EGD was performed with the patient under conscious sedation. The endoscope was inserted orally and advanced through the esophagus into the stomach and duodenum. Visualization revealed multiple erosions in the gastric mucosa with active oozing, suggestive of gastritis with acute bleeding. Hemostasis was achieved using thermal coagulation.

Impression:

EGD findings consistent with acute gastritis and active bleeding from gastric erosions.

Post-procedure Care:

Following hemostasis, the patient was closely monitored for signs of recurrent bleeding or complications. Proton pump inhibitors were initiated to suppress gastric acid secretion and promote healing of the erosions. Intravenous fluids were administered to maintain hydration, and blood transfusion was considered if hemoglobin levels remained low.

Follow-up:

A follow-up EGD was scheduled to assess healing of the gastric mucosa and evaluate for any underlying causes of gastritis, such as Helicobacter pylori infection or medication-induced injury.
