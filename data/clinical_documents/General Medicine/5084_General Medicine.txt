Chief Complaint:
The patient presents with recurrent abdominal pain and bloating.

History of Present Illness:
A 22-year-old female reports experiencing intermittent episodes of abdominal discomfort for the past six months. She describes the pain as crampy and diffuse, often accompanied by bloating and occasional episodes of diarrhea. The symptoms tend to worsen after meals, especially those high in fat or fiber. She denies any history of nausea, vomiting, or significant weight changes. She has tried over-the-counter antacids and dietary modifications with minimal relief.

Past Medical History:
No significant past medical history reported. No history of gastrointestinal disorders or surgeries.

Family History:
Negative for gastrointestinal disorders.

Review of Systems:
Negative for fever, rash, or other constitutional symptoms. No respiratory or genitourinary symptoms reported.

Physical Examination:
Vital Signs: Within normal limits.
Gastrointestinal: Abdomen soft and non-tender with no palpable masses or organomegaly. No rebound tenderness or guarding appreciated. Bowel sounds normal.

Assessment:

Functional Gastrointestinal Disorder: Suspected diagnosis based on the patient's history of recurrent abdominal pain and bloating without evidence of organic pathology.
Differential Diagnosis: Consideration given to conditions such as irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) or functional dyspepsia.
Plan:

Symptom Diary:
Recommend keeping a detailed diary of symptoms, including triggers and exacerbating factors, to aid in diagnosis and management.

Dietary Counseling: 
Refer the patient to a registered dietitian for personalized dietary recommendations tailored to her symptoms and nutritional needs.

Pharmacotherapy: 
Consider initiating medications such as antispasmodics or probiotics for symptom management, based on the severity and frequency of symptoms.

Stress Management: 
Encourage stress reduction techniques such as mindfulness meditation or relaxation exercises to help alleviate symptoms.

Follow-up: 
Schedule a follow-up appointment in four weeks to assess response to treatment and adjust management as needed.
