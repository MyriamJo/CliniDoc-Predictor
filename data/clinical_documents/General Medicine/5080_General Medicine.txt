Chief Complaint:
The parent presents with concerns regarding a skin rash on their child.

History of Present Illness:
A 3-year-old child is brought in by their parent, who reports noticing a rash on the child's face and body for the past three days. The rash consists of small, red, raised bumps that are mildly pruritic. The parent denies any recent illnesses, insect bites, or exposure to new skincare products or detergents. The child has had no fever, respiratory symptoms, or changes in appetite or behavior.

Past Medical History:
No significant past medical history reported.

Family History:
Negative for dermatological disorders.

Social History:
The child lives in a clean and hygienic environment. There are no known exposures to infectious diseases or environmental allergens.

Review of Systems:
Negative for fever, chills, joint pain, or gastrointestinal symptoms. No history of urinary complaints.

Physical Examination:
Vital Signs: Within normal limits for age.
Skin: Diffuse erythematous rash with scattered papules observed on the face, trunk, and extremities. Lesions are non-vesicular and non-blanching. No signs of infection or purulent discharge noted.

Assessment:

Viral Exanthem: Differential diagnosis includes a viral rash such as roseola or erythema infectiosum.
Allergic Reaction: Consideration for allergic contact dermatitis or urticaria due to environmental triggers.
Plan:

Symptomatic Treatment: 
Recommend topical corticosteroid cream for pruritus relief and oral antihistamines for symptomatic relief.

Observation: 
Instruct the parent to monitor the rash for any changes and to follow up if symptoms persist or worsen.

Education: 
Provide guidance on skincare practices and avoidance of potential allergens or irritants.

Follow-up: 
Schedule a follow-up appointment in one week for reassessment and further management as indicated.
