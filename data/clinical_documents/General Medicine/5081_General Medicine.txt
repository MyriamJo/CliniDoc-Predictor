Chief Complaint:
The patient presents with persistent knee pain and difficulty walking.

History of Present Illness:
A 25-year-old male reports experiencing gradual onset of left knee pain over the past six months. He describes the pain as dull and achy, primarily located on the medial aspect of the knee, exacerbated by weight-bearing activities and prolonged standing. He denies any history of trauma or recent injuries to the knee. Additionally, he complains of stiffness and occasional swelling around the joint. The pain has progressively worsened, limiting his ability to walk and participate in physical activities.

Past Medical History:
No significant past medical history reported. No history of previous knee surgeries or chronic joint conditions.

Family History:
Negative for musculoskeletal disorders.

Social History:
The patient leads an active lifestyle, regularly engaging in sports and recreational activities. He denies smoking or alcohol consumption.

Review of Systems:
Negative for fever, rash, or other joint pain. No gastrointestinal or respiratory symptoms reported.

Physical Examination:
Vital Signs: Within normal limits.
Musculoskeletal: Inspection reveals mild swelling and tenderness over the left medial knee joint. Limited range of motion noted with flexion and extension. No palpable crepitus or joint instability appreciated.

Assessment:

Knee Pain:
Suspected to be related to an underlying musculoskeletal condition such as patellofemoral pain syndrome or early osteoarthritis.
Mechanical Dysfunction: Consideration given to malalignment or biomechanical abnormalities contributing to the symptoms.
Plan:

Diagnostic Imaging: 
Recommend obtaining X-rays of the left knee to evaluate for any structural abnormalities or signs of osteoarthritis.

Physical Therapy:
Referral to a physical therapist for targeted exercises to improve knee strength, flexibility, and biomechanics.

Pain Management: 
Prescribe nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for pain relief and to reduce inflammation.

Activity Modification: 
Advise the patient to avoid high-impact activities and to incorporate rest periods during periods of pain exacerbation.

Follow-up: 
Schedule a follow-up appointment in four weeks to assess response to treatment and consider further interventions if necessary.
