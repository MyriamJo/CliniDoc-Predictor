Medical Record:

Procedure: Gallbladder Ultrasound (Sonography)

Reason for Procedure:

Right upper quadrant abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting, suggestive of possible gallbladder pathology.

Ultrasound Findings:

The gallbladder ultrasound was performed to assess the patient's symptoms. The examination revealed the following findings:

1. Gallstones: Multiple hyperechoic structures were visualized within the gallbladder lumen, consistent with gallstones. The size and number of stones varied, with some measuring up to 1.5 centimeters in diameter.

2. Gallbladder Wall Thickness: The gallbladder wall appeared thickened, measuring approximately 6 millimeters, which is indicative of acute inflammation, likely secondary to cholecystitis.

3. Pericholecystic Fluid: There was evidence of pericholecystic fluid accumulation, suggesting inflammation extending beyond the gallbladder wall, which is a common feature of acute cholecystitis.

Impression:

The gallbladder ultrasound findings are consistent with cholelithiasis (gallstones) and acute cholecystitis. The presence of gallstones, gallbladder wall thickening, and pericholecystic fluid support the diagnosis of acute inflammation of the gallbladder.

Recommendations:

1. Medical Management: Immediate consultation with a healthcare provider for further evaluation and management of acute cholecystitis. Antibiotics and analgesics may be prescribed to alleviate symptoms and manage the infection.

2. Consideration for Cholecystectomy: Surgical consultation is warranted to discuss the possibility of cholecystectomy (surgical removal of the gallbladder) to address the underlying cause of acute cholecystitis and prevent recurrent episodes.

3. Symptomatic Relief: In the interim, symptomatic relief with pain management, antiemetics for nausea and vomiting, and dietary modifications (low-fat diet) may be recommended to alleviate discomfort and reduce exacerbations of symptoms.

Follow-up:

The patient is advised to follow up closely with their healthcare provider for ongoing monitoring and management of acute cholecystitis. Urgent intervention may be necessary if symptoms worsen or if complications such as gallbladder perforation or abscess formation occur.
