HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
A 25-year-old female presents with a one-week history of progressively worsening abdominal pain, particularly in the right lower quadrant. She describes the pain as crampy and intermittent, associated with nausea and anorexia. She denies any fever, vomiting, or changes in bowel habits.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
Non-contributory.

SOCIAL HISTORY:
Works as a teacher. Non-smoker, occasional alcohol consumption.

FAMILY HISTORY:
Non-contributory.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Vital Signs: Temperature 98.4°F, Blood Pressure 120/70 mmHg, Pulse 80 bpm, Respiratory Rate 18 breaths/min.
Abdominal: Tenderness and guarding in the right lower quadrant, without rebound tenderness or rigidity. Negative McBurney's and Rovsing's signs.
Gynecological: Normal external genitalia, no cervical motion tenderness.

ASSESSMENT:

Acute Appendicitis: Suspected diagnosis based on clinical presentation and examination findings.
Gynecological Causes: Differential diagnosis includes gynecological conditions such as ovarian cysts or pelvic inflammatory disease.
PLAN:

Abdominal Imaging: Order abdominal ultrasound or CT scan for confirmation of appendicitis and evaluation of other intra-abdominal pathology.
Surgical Consultation: Arrange for surgical assessment and consideration for appendectomy.
Pain Management: Provide analgesia to alleviate abdominal pain and discomfort.
NPO Status: Advise the patient to remain NPO (nil per os) pending further evaluation and surgical consultation.
