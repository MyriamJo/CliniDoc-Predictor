Chief Complaint:

"I've been experiencing persistent pain in my upper left abdomen."

History of Present Illness:

A 40-year-old man presents with a complaint of continuous dull pain in the upper left quadrant of his abdomen for the past month. He describes the pain as aching and intermittent, sometimes radiating to his left shoulder. The pain worsens after eating and is not relieved by over-the-counter pain medications. He denies any recent trauma, fever, or changes in bowel habits. Additionally, he reports feeling fatigued and has noticed a feeling of fullness in his abdomen, especially after meals.

Past Medical History:

The patient has no significant medical history of splenic disorders or other chronic illnesses.

Family History:

Negative for significant medical conditions related to the spleen.

Social History:

The patient works as a high school teacher and leads a sedentary lifestyle. He does not smoke or consume alcohol regularly.

Medications:

Occasional use of ibuprofen for mild pain.

Allergies:

No known drug allergies.

Objective:

General: The patient appears fatigued but is alert and oriented.

Abdominal Examination: On palpation, there is tenderness and fullness noted in the left upper quadrant of the abdomen, with a palpable mass appreciated deep in the left upper abdomen. No rebound tenderness or guarding is elicited. Percussion reveals dullness over the left upper quadrant.

Assessment:

The patient is suspected to have a splenic disorder, possibly splenomegaly or a splenic mass, given the persistent left upper abdominal pain and palpable mass on examination.

Plan:

Management plan includes:
- Immediate referral to gastroenterology or hematology for further evaluation and imaging studies (e.g., ultrasound or CT scan) to assess the size and structure of the spleen.
- Laboratory investigations, including complete blood count (CBC) with differential, liver function tests, and infectious disease serologies to evaluate for underlying causes of splenic enlargement.
- Symptomatic relief with analgesics as needed for pain management.
- Advising the patient to avoid strenuous physical activities or activities that may increase the risk of trauma to the abdomen until further evaluation is completed.
- Follow-up appointment scheduled within the week to review imaging results and discuss further management options based on the diagnosis. Referral to surgery may be considered if a surgical intervention is warranted.
