HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
A 35-year-old male presents with a one-week history of persistent cough, associated with yellowish sputum production and low-grade fever. He reports feeling fatigued and experiencing mild shortness of breath on exertion. The symptoms started gradually and have been progressively worsening. He denies any chest pain, wheezing, or hemoptysis.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
Non-contributory.

SOCIAL HISTORY:
Works as a construction worker. Smokes half a pack of cigarettes daily for the past 15 years. Denies alcohol or illicit drug use.

FAMILY HISTORY:
Non-contributory.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Vital Signs: Temperature 99.2°F, Blood Pressure 120/80 mmHg, Pulse 80 bpm, Respiratory Rate 18 breaths/min.
General: Appears tired but alert.
Respiratory: Decreased breath sounds and dullness to percussion over the right lower lung field.
Cardiovascular: Regular rhythm without murmurs.
Abdominal: Soft and non-tender.

ASSESSMENT:

Right Lower Lobe Pneumonia: Suspected diagnosis based on clinical presentation and physical examination findings.
Smoking History: Significant smoking history predisposing to respiratory infections.
PLAN:

Chest X-ray: Order for confirmation of pneumonia and assessment of severity.
Antibiotic Therapy: Initiate empiric antibiotic treatment with amoxicillin-clavulanate.
Smoking Cessation: Provide counseling and resources for smoking cessation.
Symptomatic Management: Recommend rest, hydration, and over-the-counter cough suppressants.
