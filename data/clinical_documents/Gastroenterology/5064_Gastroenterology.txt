Medical Record:

Procedure: Gallbladder Ultrasound (Sonography)

Reason for Procedure:

Recurrent episodes of right upper quadrant abdominal pain, especially after consuming fatty meals, and occasional nausea, suggestive of possible gallbladder dysfunction or biliary colic.

Ultrasound Findings:

The gallbladder ultrasound was performed to assess the patient's symptoms. The examination revealed the following findings:

1. Gallstones: Multiple echogenic structures were visualized within the gallbladder lumen, consistent with gallstones. The size and number of stones varied, with some measuring up to 1.2 centimeters in diameter.

2. Gallbladder Wall Thickness: The gallbladder wall appeared thickened, measuring approximately 5 millimeters, indicative of chronic inflammation or cholecystitis.

3. No Pericholecystic Fluid: There was no evidence of pericholecystic fluid accumulation or signs of acute inflammation beyond the gallbladder wall.

Impression:

The gallbladder ultrasound findings are consistent with cholelithiasis (gallstones) and chronic cholecystitis. The presence of gallstones and gallbladder wall thickening supports the diagnosis of chronic inflammation of the gallbladder.

Recommendations:

1. Symptomatic Management: Immediate consultation with a healthcare provider for symptomatic relief and management of biliary colic. Analgesics and dietary modifications (low-fat diet) may be recommended to alleviate symptoms.

2. Consideration for Cholecystectomy: Surgical consultation is warranted to discuss the possibility of cholecystectomy (surgical removal of the gallbladder) to address chronic cholecystitis and prevent recurrent episodes of biliary colic.

3. Lifestyle Modifications: Advising the patient to avoid fatty or greasy foods that may exacerbate symptoms and to maintain a healthy lifestyle with regular exercise and weight management.

Follow-up:

The patient is advised to follow up closely with their healthcare provider for ongoing monitoring and management of chronic cholecystitis. Discussion of treatment options, including the possibility of cholecystectomy, will be addressed during the follow-up appointment.
