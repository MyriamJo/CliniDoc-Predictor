Subjective:

The patient, a 7-year-old girl, is brought in by her parents with complaints of persistent itching and discomfort in her scalp. According to her parents, the itching started approximately two weeks ago and has progressively worsened. They report that the patient has been scratching her scalp frequently, especially at night, leading to disturbed sleep. The parents deny any recent changes in hair care products or exposure to potential allergens.

Past Medical History:

The patient has no significant medical history of scalp conditions or allergies.

Family History:

Negative for significant dermatological conditions.

Objective:

General: The patient appears well-nourished and in no acute distress.

Scalp Examination: Upon inspection, the scalp appears erythematous and inflamed, with evidence of excoriation and crusting. There are multiple discrete areas of hair loss, particularly along the frontal hairline and in the occipital region. No signs of active bleeding or discharge are noted.

Assessment:

The patient is diagnosed with scalp pruritus and alopecia, suggestive of a possible underlying scalp infection or infestation.

Plan:

Management plan includes:
- Scalp examination under a Wood's lamp to rule out the presence of fungal infections or head lice.
- If fungal infection is suspected, treatment with antifungal shampoo containing ketoconazole or selenium sulfide may be initiated.
- If head lice are detected, treatment with a pediculicide shampoo followed by meticulous combing to remove lice and nits.
- Symptomatic relief with topical corticosteroid cream to reduce inflammation and itching. The cream will be applied sparingly to affected areas of the scalp twice daily for one week.
- Educating the parents on proper hair care practices and emphasizing the importance of avoiding excessive scratching to prevent further irritation and potential infection.
- Follow-up appointment scheduled in two weeks to assess response to treatment and ensure resolution of symptoms. Referral to dermatology may be considered if symptoms persist or worsen despite initial management.
