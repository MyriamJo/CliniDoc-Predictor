HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
A 55-year-old female presents with chronic lower back pain persisting for the past six months. She describes the pain as dull and achy, radiating from the lower back to the buttocks and thighs. The pain worsens with prolonged sitting or standing and is partially relieved by lying down. She denies any history of trauma or recent heavy lifting. Over-the-counter pain medications provide temporary relief but fail to alleviate the pain completely.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
History of osteoarthritis and hypertension. No history of previous back surgeries.

PAST SURGICAL HISTORY:
Nonsignificant.

FAMILY HISTORY:
Negative for chronic back pain or spinal disorders.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Vital Signs: Within normal limits.
Musculoskeletal: Limited range of motion in the lumbar spine, tenderness over the lower lumbar vertebrae, no focal neurological deficits appreciated.

ASSESSMENT:

Chronic Mechanical Back Pain: Suspected diagnosis based on the patient's history of persistent lower back pain exacerbated by movement and relieved by rest.
PLAN:

Imaging Studies: Order lumbar spine X-rays or MRI to evaluate for any degenerative changes or structural abnormalities contributing to the pain.
Physical Therapy: Refer the patient to physical therapy for core strengthening exercises and flexibility training.
Pain Management: Consider prescribing nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) or muscle relaxants for pain relief, as needed.
Lifestyle Modification: Encourage proper body mechanics, weight management, and ergonomic adjustments to minimize strain on the lower back.
Follow-up: Schedule a follow-up appointment in four weeks to assess response to treatment and discuss further management options.
