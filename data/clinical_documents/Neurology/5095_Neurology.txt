Title of Procedure:
Lumbar Puncture for Evaluation of Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH)

Indication for Procedure:

A 30-year-old obese female presented with chronic headaches, pulsatile tinnitus, and visual disturbances, including transient visual obscurations. Clinical examination revealed papilledema on fundoscopic examination. Suspected diagnosis of idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH) prompted the recommendation for a lumbar puncture to measure cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) opening pressure and aid in the diagnosis and management of the condition.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Suspected idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH).

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH).

Procedure:

The patient was positioned in the seated or lateral decubitus position, and the lower back was prepped and draped in a sterile manner. Local anesthesia was administered, and a spinal needle was inserted into the subarachnoid space between the lumbar vertebrae. CSF pressure was measured, and CSF was collected for analysis.

Impression:

Lumbar puncture revealed elevated opening pressure (> 25 cm of water) in the cerebrospinal fluid, consistent with a diagnosis of idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH). Analysis of CSF constituents, including cell count, protein levels, and glucose levels, was within normal limits, ruling out infectious or inflammatory etiologies.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was started on acetazolamide for symptomatic relief and reduction of intracranial pressure. Weight management and lifestyle modifications, including dietary changes and sodium restriction, were recommended to improve long-term outcomes. Close ophthalmological follow-up was arranged to monitor papilledema resolution and visual function.
