Reason for Visit:

A 55-year-old woman seeks medical attention due to persistent feelings of fatigue and difficulty concentrating.

History of Present Illness:

The patient reports experiencing ongoing fatigue and lack of energy for the past few months, which has been interfering with her daily activities. She describes feeling physically and mentally exhausted, often struggling to stay awake during the day despite getting adequate sleep at night. She notes that her fatigue is not relieved by rest and tends to worsen as the day progresses.

In addition to fatigue, the patient complains of difficulty concentrating and memory problems. She finds it challenging to focus on tasks at work and frequently forgets important details. These cognitive difficulties have been affecting her job performance and causing frustration.

The patient's sleep patterns have been disrupted, with difficulty falling asleep and frequent awakenings throughout the night. She describes her sleep as restless, often waking up feeling unrefreshed and groggy in the morning. She denies any history of snoring or witnessed apneic episodes during sleep.

During the daytime, the patient admits to relying on caffeinated beverages to combat fatigue and stay alert. She consumes multiple cups of coffee throughout the day, but finds that the caffeine provides only temporary relief. She also reports craving sugary snacks and carbohydrates, which she consumes in large quantities to boost her energy levels.

The patient leads a sedentary lifestyle, spending most of her time sitting at her desk for work or watching television at home. She admits to occasional cigarette smoking, particularly during times of stress.

Past Medical History:

1. Hypothyroidism.
2. Depression.
3. Fibromyalgia.

Family History:

Positive for autoimmune disorders and mood disorders.

Social History:

The patient is married with two adult children. She works full-time as an office manager in a high-stress environment.

Review of Systems:

Not contributory.

Medications:

The patient takes the following medications:
1. Levothyroxine for hypothyroidism.
2. Sertraline for depression.
3. Gabapentin for fibromyalgia.

Findings:

Vital Signs: Blood pressure 130/80 mmHg, pulse 70 bpm, respiratory rate 16 bpm, weight 170 lbs, height 5 feet 6 inches, temperature 98.6°F. HEENT: Normocephalic and atraumatic. Pupils equal, round, and reactive to light. Nasal passages clear. Oropharynx without erythema or exudates. Neck: Supple without lymphadenopathy or masses. Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs. Lungs: Clear to auscultation bilaterally. Abdomen: Soft, nontender, nondistended. Extremities: No edema, normal range of motion.

Laboratory and Imaging:

Routine blood work shows no significant abnormalities.

Assessment and Plan:

The patient's symptoms are consistent with a combination of fatigue, cognitive dysfunction, and sleep disturbance, likely stemming from her underlying medical conditions and lifestyle factors.

To address the fatigue and cognitive difficulties, I will optimize management of her hypothyroidism and depression, ensuring that her thyroid hormone levels are within the therapeutic range and considering adjustments to her antidepressant medication if necessary. Additionally, I will explore strategies to improve sleep quality, including implementing sleep hygiene practices and considering referral to a sleep specialist for further evaluation of possible sleep disorders such as insomnia or sleep apnea.

Regarding her reliance on caffeine and unhealthy eating habits, I will provide education on the importance of a balanced diet and encourage moderation in caffeine consumption. I will also recommend incorporating regular physical activity into her daily routine to help combat fatigue and improve overall well-being.

Follow-up appointments will be scheduled to monitor the patient's progress and adjust treatment as needed. Referral to other specialists, such as a nutritionist or behavioral therapist, may be considered to provide additional support.
