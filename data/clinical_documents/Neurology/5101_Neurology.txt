Title of Procedure: 
Lumbar Puncture for Evaluation of Multiple Sclerosis

Indication for Procedure:

A 30-year-old male presented with recurrent episodes of numbness, weakness, and visual disturbances. Clinical evaluation raised suspicion for multiple sclerosis (MS), an autoimmune demyelinating disorder of the central nervous system. Lumbar puncture was recommended to assess cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) for the presence of oligoclonal bands and aid in the diagnosis of MS.

Preprocedure Diagnosis:

Suspected multiple sclerosis (MS).

Postprocedure Diagnosis:

Multiple sclerosis (MS).

Procedure:

The patient was positioned in the lateral decubitus position, and the lumbar region was prepped and draped in a sterile manner. After local anesthesia, a spinal needle was inserted into the subarachnoid space, and CSF was collected and sent for analysis. CSF samples were evaluated for the presence of oligoclonal bands using immunoelectrophoresis.

Impression:

Lumbar puncture revealed the presence of oligoclonal bands in the cerebrospinal fluid, consistent with a diagnosis of multiple sclerosis (MS). The findings supported the clinical suspicion of MS, corroborating the patient's presentation of recurrent neurological symptoms.

Postoperative Care:

The patient was referred to a neurologist specializing in multiple sclerosis for further evaluation and management. Treatment options, including disease-modifying therapies such as immunomodulators and symptomatic management, were discussed based on the lumbar puncture findings. Close monitoring of disease progression and treatment response was instituted, with plans for regular follow-up appointments to assess disease activity and adjust therapy as needed.
